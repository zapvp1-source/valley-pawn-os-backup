## PORSCHE — SOLD JUNE 2024, and the filed return doesn't know it

**Confirmed 8/4/2026:** the DuPont loan payoff of **$75,012.65 appears on the June 2024 DuPont statement.** Purchased 1/3/2024, gone by June 2024.

### What the corrected 2024 numbers look like

The governing rule: **property placed in service and disposed of in the same tax year gets no depreciation at all.** Bought January, sold June, same year — so:

| | As filed | Corrected |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 depreciation | $17,497 | **$0** — disallowed |
| Basis at sale | — | $97,376 |
| Amount realized | — | ~$70,000 |
| Total loss | — | ~$27,376 |
| **Deductible §1231 loss** (85.77% business) | — | **~$23,481** |
| Personal portion (14.23%) | — | ~$3,895, **not deductible** |

**Net 2024 effect: roughly $6,000 of additional deduction** (swap out $17,497 of depreciation, swap in ~$23,481 of §1231 loss) — worth ~$1,900 at a 32% marginal rate.

**Net 2025 effect: the $19,800 of projected Porsche depreciation has to come out**, which *increases* 2025 income by $19,800 — roughly $6,300 of tax.

**So fixing this costs about $4,400 net across the two years.** Be clear-eyed: this is not a refund opportunity. It's a correction. The reason to do it is that the alternative is claiming $19,800 of depreciation on the 2025 return for a car sold in June 2024, with a DuPont payoff statement in the file proving exactly when it left.
