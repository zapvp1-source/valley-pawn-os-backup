## Section 6 — Ambiguities and Risk Flags (read before filing)

### 6.1 The one documented item is from 2022 — over three years before the rental conversion
The Red Rock wall was contracted **March 2022** and the property was not converted to rental until **~August 2025**. This matters:

- A capital improvement made during the personal-residence period **does** add to the property's adjusted basis. It is not lost.
- **However**, on conversion of a personal residence to rental use, the **depreciable basis is the LESSER of (a) adjusted basis at conversion or (b) FMV at the date of conversion** (Treas. Reg. §1.168(i)-4; Pub. 527). You cannot simply add $21,172 of 2022 spending on top of a 2025 basis figure and depreciate it.
- **Action:** the cost-seg provider needs (i) the original purchase price and closing statement, (ii) a defensible **FMV at August 2025**, and (iii) the improvement schedule — then apply the lesser-of test. Confirm the intake package reflects this. Two intake drafts exist in Drive (`Bald_Rock_Cost_Seg_Intake_Package.docx` `1WHwuAKTFWHc_6H_dovDotO9_wGVTsLAh`; `Bald_Rock_Cost_Seg_Intake_Package_v2_CORRECTED_BASIS.docx` `17KiD-9OiZU9_AvHL-dSTY8OkOoR-dSKZ`) — the "CORRECTED_BASIS" filename suggests this issue was already flagged once. Neither file's contents were machine-readable in this review; **open v2 and verify the basis methodology before the cost seg is run.**

### 6.2 Repairs (currently deductible) vs. capital improvements (basis additions)
The distinction is not cosmetic — the IRS tangible property regulations (§1.263(a)-3) require capitalization only where the work is a **Betterment, Adaptation, or Restoration** of a unit of property. Items in this file that likely fall on the **repair/deductible** side, not basis:

- **`282 Pool Repair.pdf`** — the title says *repair*. A pool repair that merely restores existing function is a deductible expense, not a basis addition. Do not let this land in the cost-seg schedule without reading it.
- **`282 Plumbing.pdf`** — same concern. Plumbing *repair* = expense; plumbing *replacement/re-pipe/new fixture rough-in* = capital.
- **HVAC** — replacing an entire HVAC system is generally a **Restoration** (major component) and therefore capital, so the HVAC quotes would qualify **if payment is proven**. But note the **de minimis safe harbor** and the **Small Taxpayer Safe Harbor for Buildings** may make some items deductible instead — coordinate with the CPA.
- **Aprilaire humidifier ($1,890–$2,825 quoted)** — small addition; may be more efficiently expensed under the de minimis safe harbor than capitalized.

### 6.3 Landscaping: land improvement vs. non-depreciable land
The Shreckhise trees and shrubs (`282 bushes.pdf`) are a classic cost-seg gray area. **Ornamental plantings immediately adjacent to and dependent on the building** can sometimes be treated as 15-year §1250 land improvements; general landscaping and trees **not** tied to the building are typically **added to non-depreciable land basis** and recover nothing. Flag this to the cost-seg engineer rather than assuming 15-year treatment.

### 6.4 Personal-use / mixed-use exposure
Multiple documents show **282 Bald Rock Road used as the business address of Full Circle Finance Inc (DBA Valley Pawn)** — e.g., the Expansion Capital Group adverse action notice (thread `19e3d580a417a30d`, 2026-05-18, "Business Address: 282 BALD ROCK ROAD, VERONA, VA 24482") and Rapid Finance (thread `19e1d253974965ef`, 2026-05-12). Confirm 2025 personal-use days.
