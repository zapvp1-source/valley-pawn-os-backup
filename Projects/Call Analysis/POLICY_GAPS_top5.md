# Top 5 policy gaps from the call review — for Joshua's review

These are the five that are **policy problems, not people problems**. Each one showed up across
multiple employees at multiple stores in a single week, which means coaching one person won't fix
it. Evidence is from the Aug 31–Sep 6 recordings; call numbers refer to
`CONDUCT_REVIEW_2026-08-31_week.md`.

Each is written so you can approve, edit, or kill it. Once you sign off, these can go through the
normal policy flow — drafted, published, e-signed in Gusto, and added to the manual.

---

## 1. Verify before you disclose — anything

**The gap:** we have no verification standard on the phone. A last name gets you a full payoff
amount and an itemized list of everything on the account.

**What it looked like:**
- A caller gave the last name "Morris" and received the payoff plus every item — PlayStation,
  Switch, bracelet, tablet, computer, controller, camera, jewelry. He referred to the borrower as
  *"she."* He was not the account holder. (Call 68, HAR)
- A caller said "Vincent Robinson," then "Vincent Robertson" seconds later. The mismatch was
  never resolved; due date, delinquency and payoff were given anyway. (Call 108, HAR)
- Payoff figures relayed to third parties calling on someone's behalf. (Calls 143, 151, WAY)

**Proposed standard:** name plus one non-public identifier (loan/ticket number, DOB, or the last
four of the phone on file) before any balance, due date, item description, or account status
leaves the building. No exceptions for "they sounded like they knew the account." If verification
fails: "I can't go over account details on the phone, but come in with your ID and we'll take
care of it."

## 2. Firearms are never confirmed as being in the store

**The gap:** this is #1's most dangerous special case and deserves its own line, because it's a
security issue on top of a privacy one.

**What it looked like:**
- Caller self-identified as "James Brown," nothing else. Staff confirmed the exact model on pawn
  and that it was physically present: *"And it's Glock 30 Gen 5. Yes, sir. It is. Yeah, that's
  still here."* (Call 156, WAY)
- Similar shape on a Glock-backed loan at HAR (Call 211) and firearm due dates read out with no
  verification step at WAY (Call 195).

**Proposed standard:** we never confirm over the phone that a specific firearm is on the
premises, is on a loan, or belongs to a named person — to anyone, verified or not. Redemption and
status conversations happen in person with ID. Background-check status is the one exception, to
the buyer only, after verification.

## 3. No precious-metal quote by phone without the spot sheet in hand

**The gap:** the melt-calculation policy exists and was signed, but it isn't reaching the phone.

**What it looked like:** ~2.5 oz of US gold coins quoted at *"very very roughly around 4,500"* —
with *"I'm kind of spitballin' here"* said out loud to the customer — followed by *"I really
don't see myself paying exactly spot."* The customer said he'd been offered around $10,000 and
the lead is almost certainly gone. Nobody identified the item, which appears to be the 1980–84
US Mint gold medallion series, a standard catalogued piece. (Call 204, HAR)

**Proposed standard:** gold, silver, platinum and diamonds get no number over the phone unless
the employee has the current spot sheet open and the item identified. Otherwise: "I don't want to
guess and cost you money — bring it in and we'll weigh and test it while you wait." Anything over
a set threshold (suggest 1 oz of gold or ~$2,500) goes to Preston before a number is spoken.

## 4. The sell-side call standard — a number or an appointment, every time

**This is the money one.** Across 256 calls, essentially every caller who got a number said they
were coming in. Almost none of the callers told "text me photos" did — and we took no name, so
they're simply gone.

**What it looked like:**
- Nine Nintendo Switch games: *"it all depends on the game."* Staff never asked a single title.
- A seller with three Makita drills and a saw **sitting in our parking lot** was asked to text
  photos instead, because the employee was alone.
- An ASUS ROG Ally: repeated holds, no number, "text me pictures," no name taken.
- Contrast: a crossbow quoted a conditional $160 — *"it's just a rough estimate"* — and the
  caller said he'd be in before six. That's the whole technique.

**Proposed standard:** no sell-side call ends without one of three things — a conditional range,
a booked time, or a name and callback number. Conditional ranges are explicitly allowed and
encouraged ("roughly $X to $Y depending on condition, I'd need to see it"). Also: a want-list at
every store for anything we didn't have in stock, since we currently capture zero of those and
never check the other four stores.

## 5. What staff may not say to a customer

**The gap:** there is no rule about discussing internal matters on the phone, and the absence
showed up repeatedly and expensively.

**What it looked like:**
- A customer was told the Harrisonburg store manager had been fired, told which employee was
  transferring back, and told that employee *"talks about you all the time."* Customer: *"Yeah,
  I'm gonna quit coming in there."* (Call 249, WAY)
- *"This is coming from the owner and the general manager... I'm the reason why they haven't been
  expired. I've been trying to watch out for you."* (Call 239, LEX)
- An upset warranty customer told *"I don't think showing up's really going to help the
  situation... with Preston not being here."* (Call 159, HAR)
- Two employees on an open recorded line speculating a customer sells drugs and that another
  brought in a cart of stolen retail goods. (Call 63, HAR)
- Store cash position volunteered to an unknown caller: *"I ain't got much to play with today."*
  (Call 212, HAR) — and *"I'm by myself today"* said to a caller sitting in the parking lot
  (Call 129, LEX).

**Proposed standard:** never name the owner, the GM, or another employee to a customer as the
reason for anything — the answer is "our policy is," full stop. Never disclose terminations,
staffing changes, who is or isn't working, staffing levels, or cash on hand. Never speculate
about a customer's character or conduct on any line. And assume every line is recorded, because
it is.

## Two smaller ones worth folding in rather than making their own policy

- **Voicemail script.** Loan status and expiration threats are being left on boxes with generic
  carrier greetings that never confirm whose phone it is — a pattern, mostly Lexington (Calls 29,
  81, 125, 130, 137, 230, 234). One line fixes it: name, store, callback number, nothing else.
- **Appraisal tools.** A 14-minute call spent reading Gemini and ChatGPT output to a customer,
  ending in a pass (Call 93, WAY). eBay comps are fine and staff use them well; consumer AI
  chatbots as the valuation method, live on the phone, are not.
