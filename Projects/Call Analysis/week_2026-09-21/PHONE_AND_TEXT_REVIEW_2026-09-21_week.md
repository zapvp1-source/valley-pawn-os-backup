## 2. What the calls were about

Of the 368 recordings:
- **279 were real customer conversations.**
- 59 were silent or just the recording notice.
- 20 were outbound voicemails, mostly past-due outreach.
- The rest were internal, vendor, wrong-number or spam calls. Spam is essentially gone: 1 robocall all week.

**Top reasons customers called:**
1. **Wanting to sell something: 69.** Harrisonburg took 29 of these.
2. **Loan payments or extensions: 58.** Many pushed to payday, with a wave of worry about the Oct 1 75-day change.
3. **Firearms: 36 calls touched guns.** These were buying, pawning, transfer fees and "your background check cleared" pickups.
4. **Price and availability checks: 23.** Plus 22 new pawn-loan requests and 12 gold and jewelry calls.

**Top categories:** guns (36), gold and jewelry (24), game consoles (22), tools (19), outdoor and sporting (14), audio (9).

**Results:**
- 81 calls ended with a deal or the customer coming in.
- 71 were answered with info only.
- 48 had a payment or extension handled.
- 27 ended with a callback promised.
- 10 got no help at all.

### Asked for, but we didn't have it (13)
- **Game consoles, the steadiest ask:** PS5s (Lexington sold out, Culpeper had none), Xbox Series S at Culpeper, and a "PS" console at Waynesboro.
- **Heaters, the new seasonal signal:** Waynesboro and Lexington both got calls and had none.
- **Guns:** a 3.5" semi-auto shotgun at Culpeper (all 14 in stock are pumps), a 20-gauge and a Glock 29 at Harrisonburg.
- **Other:** bowling balls (Harrisonburg, twice), boxing gear, a car amp, a propane tailgate grill, and DVDs.

### What we turned away (18) or lost on price (10)
- **Waynesboro has shut off general merchandise.** A staffer told a regular the store is "backlogged" and only taking prior-business items, guns, gold, and newer consoles. So no collectibles, antiques, tools, soundbars, car audio, hitches or Fire Sticks. If that isn't a decision you made, it's costing buys.
- **Phones and smartwatches:**
  - Culpeper and Waynesboro told callers "we don't do phones at all."
  - Galaxy Watch, Q7 watch and first-gen Google Home were turned away.
- **TVs at Harrisonburg:** one repeat caller made 5 calls and was declined on multiple TVs (no stand, leaning legs).
- **Car audio:** Lexington and Culpeper ("we don't really do car stuff").
- **Other turn-aways:**
  - a PS5 with a broken HDMI port
  - a laptop with a broken charging port
  - a power washer (no water hookup to test it)
  - 1996 brass collector coins
  - graded loose gemstones
  - e-bike and motorcycle loans
- **Lost on price:**
  - EOTech optic: seller wanted $800, we offered about $450.
  - JBL PartyBox: seller wanted $200, we offered $150.
  - Console buyer offered $400 against our $489–499 tag, with no counter.
  - PS4 Slims: $30 each.

### Procedure
- **Preston's three qualifying questions** (prior business, loan or sell, how much do you need) were asked on **8 of 81** sell or pawn calls where it could be judged, about 10%.
- **Loan offered as an alternative to selling:** only **4 of 91** sell or pawn calls, even on turn-aways.
- **Good habit spreading:** asking customers to text photos before they drive in.
