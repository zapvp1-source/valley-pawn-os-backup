# The good ones — recorded calls, Aug 31–Sep 6, 2026

Same 256 calls as the conduct review, read for the opposite thing. These are the calls worth
playing to the team. Several are better training material than anything you could script, because
they're your own people, your own customers, and the customer's reaction is right there on the
tape.

Names appear only where the employee identified themselves on the call. Transcription is
automated — dollar figures in particular get garbled, so trust the audio over the quote.

## The five to play at a team meeting

### 1. The crossbow call — how to quote without seeing the item
**Call 25 · Aug 31 · Lexington · 3m05s**

A guy called to sell a crossbow, then let slip he didn't really want to part with it. The employee
*caught that* and turned a sale into a loan — which is the better outcome for both sides. Then he
did the three things almost everyone else skips: cited real comps out loud, explained why he
couldn't be exact (no case, fewer arrows than the listings), and pre-framed the number as
conditional so nobody feels bait-and-switched at the counter. Closed by giving his hours, which is
what turned a quote into a visit.

- Customer: *"Or if I could pawn it and get a little bit higher, I can come back and get it because I wholeheartedly don't want to sell it."* → *"Yeah, we could possibly do a loan on it if that's what you want."*
- *"Now it would be based off of how this looks. So it's just like a rough estimate."*
- *"I'm up until six today if you want to stop on by."*

**This is the answer to the biggest revenue problem in the whole review.** Everyone else says "text
me photos" and loses the customer. He gave a hedged number and got the visit.

### 2. The anxious past-due call — the one your stores get most
**Call 70 · Sep 1 · Harrisonburg · 2m32s**

Woman past her due date, scared. He refused to answer from memory, pulled the account, counted the
grace days out loud so she could follow along, named the late-fee formula *and* put it in
perspective, then answered the question she was actually calling about.

- *"Let's see what we got going on here and I'll give you the exact answer to your question. Cause I don't want to tell you wrong."*
- *"So that'd be one, two, three. So actually the fifth day will be Wednesday..."*
- *"No, we wouldn't do anything that early in it anyway. I wouldn't pull it at five days. You're good."*

Make this the standard script for that call. It's the most common anxious call you get.

### 3. The collections call that ended in a thank-you
**Call 121 · Sep 3 · Lexington · 1m18s**

Chasing two past-due loans — a Lego set already a week past expiration, plus tools. He itemized
each loan, gave the catch-up figure for each and combined, and *volunteered unprompted* that her
payment wouldn't buy as much time as she thought. When she proposed splitting payments across two
days for payday reasons, he worked with it and flagged the late-fee risk honestly instead of
glossing over it to close the money.

- *"would be 55 50 but it'd be due again in about a week"*
- *"Monday it might be considered late so you might have a late fee, I'd have to double check"*
- Customer: *"I can make my Lego payment today so they don't do anything with them, because that's my baby right there."*

He heard that the item mattered to her and got her in before six the same day. Collections without
shame.

### 4. Teaching a customer how pawn interest actually works
**Call 67 · Sep 1 · Harrisonburg · 4m01s**

Customer wanted $1,600 against a PSA-10 Charizard. He declined the number with a real reason
instead of a flat no, offered a path (add the Gengar, get GM approval), then walked the customer
through the thing most borrowers never understand — that the monthly interest doesn't touch
principal, and anything above it does. He kept checking comprehension instead of talking over him.

- *"It's 10% on $1,600 is gonna be $160 a month to extend it and that doesn't go towards principal."*
- Customer: *"the hundred dollars would make it go down a hundred dollars."* → *"You're right... Yes, anything above that 160."*

**One coaching note if you play it:** he calls the interest a "storage fee." Under Virginia pawn
statute those are separate line items — right teaching, wrong word.

### 5. Service recovery — the customer who'd been accused the day before
**Call 88 · Sep 1 · Waynesboro · 4m42s**

This is the same call that's in the conduct review, and that's exactly why it's worth playing. The
customer had been questioned at the counter about whether a guitar and a Louis Armstrong award
were really his. He called back upset. The employee let him vent without interrupting, apologized,
checked with a colleague rather than guessing — and then, when the customer admitted he's bad at
haggling, **volunteered the store's actual buy ranges unprompted.** He protected a customer who
had just told him he was easy to take advantage of.

- Customer: *"I'm not a great haggler... I hold the value of like what things mean to me as a person."*
- Employee: *"I can tell you about what we take them in for... on just average PS4s right now we're about $40 for a full setup. On the pros, like 60ish."*
- Customer: *"Thank you guys so much. Just for even letting me come back."*

Play the recovery. Handle the original counter incident separately.

## Also excellent

**Call 232 · Sep 5 · Lexington · 6m50s — the hardest kind of no.** Nearly seven minutes with a

He said no clearly and early, explained why, then did the man's homework for him — Rockbridge
Music, music schools that teach lessons, Marketplace, eBay, TikTok Shop — and shared his own
experience selling t-shirts to make the advice land. Customer: *"Thank you so much. That was very
encouraging."* A firm no that ends in gratitude is the hardest thing on this list to teach.

**Call 238 · Sep 5 · Lexington · 33s — noticed and called.** Customer left a laptop on the counter.
He called her before she'd gotten far enough to miss it and offered to secure and tag it without
being asked twice. She'd been crying on the way out over a ring. Thirty-three seconds.

**Call 195 · Sep 4 · Waynesboro · 1m36s — corrected the customer in the customer's favor.** He had
two due dates wrong and would have shown up on the wrong day. The employee stopped him, walked
both loans separately, and reframed the date so it would stick: *"So three Saturdays from now."*
Customer: *"I'm writing it down. Thank you... you looking out."*

**Call 173 · Sep 4 · Harrisonburg · 2m29s — turned a vague ask into the right product.** Customer
invented his own workaround: "instead of me picking up, can I put money down and hold it a month."
Instead of yes/no, the employee named the actual product (an extension), quoted it, and gave the
new due date. Customer came in expecting to scrape together a full redemption and left owing $12.

**Call 142 · Sep 3 · Waynesboro · 1m23s — shame defusal in 90 seconds.** Customer opens embarrassed
and disorganized, can't find her paperwork. *"It's okay. So you're just a hair past due on all your
loans here. It's nothing that's like, oh man, that's absolutely terrible. But if you could come in
and make a payment of $44 and 40 cent, we can get y'all squared away."* Short enough for a huddle.

**Call 45 · Aug 31 · Waynesboro · 1m27s — removed the fear of coming in.** Woman with loose melee
diamonds and some rings, unsure if it was worth the trip. He named the actual criteria, said the
worst case out loud so she couldn't be embarrassed — *"worst thing, you know, I could just say no"*
— then noticed she'd said the stones had no sentimental value but the rings might, and offered a
loan on the rings specifically. That's listening, not scripting.

**Call 90 · Sep 1 · Waynesboro · 1m19s — the stuffed elephant.** Dad calls about his son's comfort
toy left behind. Not only did we have it, the employee volunteered that a blue fish had been left
too, which the dad hadn't even mentioned.

**Call 223 · Sep 5 · Lexington · Uriah — worth naming for a different reason.** It's in the conduct
file as an open action item, but Uriah's own conduct on it is the opposite of a problem. He's held
a sellable firearm off the floor indefinitely rather than risk it, stayed polite through being
bounced between two agencies who each dropped the ball, and kept pushing on his own initiative.
*"I also don't want to see it get sold... and then they come looking for it."* That's dead
inventory sitting there out of pure compliance discipline.

## The honest caveat

About 40% of the week's recordings captured no conversation — the greeting looping while someone
waited. Several of those are multi-minute calls. There is almost certainly more good work in there
than this list shows, and nobody should read a short list as the full picture of who's doing well.

## The through-line worth saying out loud to the team

The best calls all do the same thing: **give the customer something concrete before asking them for
anything.** A number, a date, a count of the grace days, a name for the product they're describing.
The weak calls all withhold — "depends," "I'd have to see it," "text me photos" — and the customer
hangs up with nothing to act on. That's the whole difference, and your own people are already
demonstrating it.
