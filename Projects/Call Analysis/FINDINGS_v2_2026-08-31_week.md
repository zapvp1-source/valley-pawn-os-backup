## 1. Compliance — the material exposure

### Account information given out with no verification. This is now the biggest pattern in the data.

Not isolated incidents. It appears at all three stores, across at least ten calls, and in several
of them the caller was demonstrably **not** the borrower.

- **Two third parties' delinquent accounts discussed with someone who was neither** (Call 104, WAY): *"I've been trying to get your mom to come in for like I said she's about four or five months late and so is your boyfriend."* Then payoff figures on a third party's ticket.
- **A wife's account read out to her husband** (Call 158, WAY, "Walker"): *"The two games under Victoria's account would be $64 and 35 cent."* — and "games" is very likely **"guns"** (the word is garbled the same way elsewhere). Pull that audio.
- **A mother's loan discussed with her son** (Call 217, HAR): collateral confirmed and extension quoted, on a phone number and a first name.
- **Full payoff plus a complete itemized collateral list on a surname alone** (Call 71, HAR) — caller referred to the borrower as *"she."*
- **Caller gave two different last names in one call** (Call 114, HAR: "Vincent Robinson" then "Vincent Robertson") and got the due date and rollover amount anyway.
- **Name lookup failed, so the employee used the phone number as the workaround** and released a combined two-loan total (Call 110, HAR).

### Firearms confirmed as being in the store, to unverified callers

- **Call 165, WAY, "Michael":** *"And it's Glock 30 Gen 5." "Yes, sir. It is. Yeah, that's still here."* Verification was the caller saying "My name is James Brown."
- **Call 221, HAR, Michael:** volunteered the collateral before the caller confirmed anything — *"is it for a Glock pistol? Yeah. All right, I'm seeing that here on my end."* Note he asks for *"her* phone number" — the account may not be the caller's.

This tells a stranger which gun is on site and who owns it.

### A stolen-property allegation handled by searching a named person

**Call 55, WAY, Martin.** A caller just released from jail said her ex had stolen and pawned her
property. Staff appear to have run a search on the named individual and reported the result back
to her. The correct handling is: take the report, refer to police, let law enforcement query the
records. We should not be confirming or denying a named person's transactions to a third party.

### Firearm transfer policy invented on the spot

**Call 120, HAR.** Delayed background check, and the employee said outright he didn't know the
store's rule and substituted his own: *"I don't know this place's policy exactly. I generally ask
that people come within a week of me running the paperwork"* and *"If it's not today, you can pick
it up tomorrow or whatever day you're free. It don't matter to me."* This is ATF-governed and it
was improvised on a recorded line.

## 2. Where the money is actually leaking

**The sell side, and it's not close.** Roughly 45 calls across the week were someone offering us
goods. The overwhelming majority ended with no number, no appointment, and no name captured.

**The single worst loss of the week — Call 22, HAR.** A seller with a 70" Samsung, a 55" Samsung,
a 40" TV, a PS5 Slim, and a self-built PC with two monitors. He stated his need outright: *"I gotta
make like at least $800 by today."* He had already called every competitor. **We gave no dollar
figure on anything** and closed with *"I'll respond to you as soon as I can. If I don't get back to
you right away, we're just really busy here."*

**The gold coins — Call 214, HAR, Walker.** Unchanged and still the most expensive single error.
~2.5 oz of US gold quoted at *"very very roughly around 4,500"* with *"I'm kind of spitballing here"*
said out loud. Seller had been offered ~$10,000. Walker's own words afterward: *"I'll be honest I
was way too low."* A **second** coin seller called the same store the same day (Call 220) and was
told *"I'll have my coin guy call you as soon as he gets free."* No callback appears anywhere.

**Televisions: 3 offered on Aug 31, 3 lost.** One was a recently bereaved woman — *"my boyfriend
passed away… I can't operate the television without him"* — declined because his Wi-Fi login was on
the set, which a factory reset clears. She was then **referred to a competitor by name.**

**A seller in the parking lot, twice.** Call 135 (LEX): *"I'm in the red Chrysler 200 sitting
outside your door. I've sold y'all stuff before."* Answer: *"you'd have to bring them in.
Unfortunately I'm by myself today,"* then *"I can't understand you, sir."*

**A $1,200 watch against a $100 loan** (Call 160, WAY). Customer living in hotels asked if $100 was
really the max. Answer: **"Yeah, you just answered that question."** No re-appraisal, no offer to
buy it outright.

**The photo-quote pipeline is a hole, not a process.** Three calls in one batch died in failed MMS
