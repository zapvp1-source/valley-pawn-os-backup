Why PEARL, and when to choose Dor instead

  • Cost: PEARL is 41% of Dor's year-1 cost and 21% of Dor's ongoing cost. Over 3 years that's $7,281 vs $25,800.
  • It does what we need: Standard has export and an API. The Bravo Data Extraction pipeline already produces daily transaction data per store, so a small new job (built alongside the existing ones, never inside them) can pull each day's door count and post a conversion number.
  • Install: nothing to wire. It sticks to the frame, runs on AA batteries and joins the store Wi-Fi.
  • The trade-off: a beam counts one body at a time. Two customers walking in side by side count as one, and kids under 54" aren't counted. Because the error is consistent, day-to-day and store-to-store trends still hold. Just don't read the conversion rate as an exact figure. Also, no sensor here removes staff trips through the door; set the daily number net of staff (for example, subtract a fixed staff-trip estimate).
  • Choose Dor instead if exact counts are worth about $6,400 a year more ($8,100 vs $1,677), or if any store door opens inward in a way that blocks a side beam. Dor mounts on top of the frame and reads groups correctly. Ask Dor for the 5-store enterprise price first.
  • Before ordering either one: get a photo of each front door (which way it opens and how wide it is). Both products need 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi with no sign-in page. Storetraffic ships free by FedEx 2-day and has a 30-day return; Dor has a 30-day money-back guarantee.

How the automatic Open sign works, and the one thing to test
  • Plug the sign into the Kasa plug and the plug into the wall. In the Kasa app (one shared login), set the schedule: ON 10:00 AM / OFF 6:00 PM on Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri, Sat for Harrisonburg, Waynesboro, Lexington and Roanoke, and Mon–Sat for Culpeper. The sign then follows store hours on its own. That's exactly what Preston's 8/11 checklist note asked for ("Open sign should be automatic turn on when we open the store").
  • Test first: leave the sign's own switch ON, then unplug it and plug it back in. If it lights up again, the smart plug will work. If it stays dark (common on signs with a push-button or a remote), the plug can't turn it on. Buy a sign with a plain rocker switch or no switch.
  • Holidays and snow days: open the app and switch the plug off or on as needed.
