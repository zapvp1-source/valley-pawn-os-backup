### What this means strategically — READ BEFORE SHOPPING AGAIN
1. **The roof, not the price, is the binding constraint.** An original 2004 roof puts this house
   outside standard-market appetite in Florida. State Farm Florida excludes roofs >15 years, so it
   will very likely decline for the same reason. Do not burn more time submitting applications
   until the roof is addressed.
2. **Kin's $1,354 renewal is therefore not a bad deal — it may be the only deal.** Kin is willing
   to write a 22-year-old roof. Re-frame the renewal decision accordingly: the question is not
   "can we beat $1,354," it is "who else will write us at all."
3. **The roof is the unlock for everything.** Replace it and the house becomes standard-market
   eligible, which opens real competition on price AND likely restores replacement-cost (rather
   than ACV) roof settlement.
4. **My Safe Florida Home pays for roof work — but only work done AFTER grant approval.** Sequence:
   free inspection -> grant application -> approval -> hire contractor -> final inspection ->
   disbursement. Roof-deck attachment, roof-to-wall connections and secondary water resistance are
   all eligible categories, with full roof-covering replacement bundled in where removal is required.
   The 2004 build date (now confirmed) clears the pre-2008 permit gate; homestead is active. The
   remaining gate to verify is Coverage A <= $700,000 — currently $595,000, so it PASSES today, but
   it will fail if the dwelling limit is raised much. **Do not increase Coverage A above $700K
   before applying for MSFH.**
5. **Get a wind mitigation inspection (OIR-B1-1802) regardless.** The impact windows/doors/garage
   are already installed and are worth a large credit that no carrier has documented yet.
