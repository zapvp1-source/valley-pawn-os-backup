## Section 1: Employee Administration

### 01.01 Hours of Operation
Each office holds the following business hours: Monday through Saturday, 10:00 AM - 6:00 PM. All personnel must be in the shop preparing to open no later than 9:30 AM. Personnel are expected to remain in the office until all customers have been served and all daily duties are completed.
Scheduled office-personnel hours should coincide with the activities and daily volume of the office (for example, it is better to schedule a doctor's appointment for a slower day such as Tuesday rather than a high-volume day such as Monday or Friday).
Employees are required to take a thirty (30) minute lunch break each day. Where more than one employee is on duty, lunch breaks must be staggered so the office remains open. Where only one employee is on duty, lunch is to be taken from 1:30 PM - 2:00 PM, and the door must be locked during this break.
Prior Division Manager approval is required if an employee needs to be out of the office for longer than one hour. If an office is not meeting company standards for growth and/or delinquency, the Division Manager may schedule additional night and weekend hours. No employee may work overtime without prior Division Manager approval.

### 01.06 Branch Visitors
Any person who is not a customer and is not employed by the company is considered a visitor to the branch. Visitors include state examiners, fire inspectors, building inspectors, police officers, and members of the media or press.
- First, obtain and verify the person's identity and nature of business in the shop.
- Second, immediately contact your Division Manager. If you cannot reach your supervisor, follow the chain of command until you have advised someone that you have a visitor in the branch.
- Do not allow the visitor behind the workstation until their identity has been verified and the Division Manager has been contacted. Local law enforcement will be allowed behind the workstation to search inventory for possible stolen items, but must identify themselves first.
- If the visitor is a member of the press or media, smile, say 'no comment,' and request they leave. Refer them to the Human Resources department at Home Office and immediately contact your Division Manager.
- Do not allow anyone to take your picture, interview customers inside the office, or discuss anything with a press/media visitor. There are no exceptions. Violations may result in disciplinary action.

### 01.07 Customer Complaints
All customer complaints must be addressed immediately. If a complaint cannot be resolved positively, contact your Division Manager for assistance; if unreachable, follow the chain of command. It is best for the company to find an internal resolution. Any threat of legal action or lawsuit must be immediately communicated to the Human Resources department.

### 01.08 Monthly Bonus Program
Pawnshop employees who manage profitable branches receive a monthly incentive bonus, paid once a month on the Friday after the 15th.
- Manager Bonus: 2.5% of total branch revenue. If the Manager hits budgeted revenue goals for the month, 100% of this bonus is paid; if the store does not hit budget, only 50% is paid.
- Pawnbroker Bonus: 5% of Gross Profit, with a minimum of $3,000 in sales required to qualify. Example: a pawnbroker with $10,100 in total sales and $5,500 in gross profit would receive a $275 bonus.
- Top Sales Bonus: employees (not managers) with the top sales across all locations receive a $200 bonus even if their store's budget qualifier is not met.
Additional qualifiers: managers must have managed their branch for the full 30-day period to be eligible; staff must be employed at the time of payout; bonus pay is subject to supervisor discretion and can be reduced or changed, including for delinquencies exceeding set standards or breach of company policy. The company reserves the right to modify this program at any time.

This policy replaces all prior PTO communications. Effective October 20, 2025.

## Section 2: Funds Control

### 02.01 General Policies
Company funds are not to be used for personal or unauthorized purposes. Company funds include petty cash, change fund, customer payments, and check stock. All cash disbursements must be properly documented and within company guidelines. Personal or unauthorized use of company funds is grounds for immediate termination.

### 02.02 Safe Policies
Pawn stores use safes to store cash, firearms, and jewelry inventory, which are to be handled only before or after store hours.

### 02.03 Bank Withdrawals
Each morning, the manager requests any necessary money using a check to the bank (Dupont Credit Union). Before leaving the bank, manually verify the money at the teller's window and confirm the teller has properly validated the withdrawal slip with the correct business date, account number, and amount.
Upon returning to the office, enter the full amount of cash into Bravo. Place enough cash in the cash drawers to start the day (approximately $275.00 per drawer to make change for customers); secure the remainder in an envelope elsewhere in the office.
Opening the till: select "Open Till," mouse over "Main Drawer," select "Remove/Add Cash," then "Add" to add cash, enter the amount, and select "add cash from bank account."
High-volume days may require additional withdrawals; contact your Division Manager, who will make the request to the owner. On days when the bank is closed but the office is open (federal holidays, Saturdays), requests for funds should be made in advance.

### 02.04 Cash Drawers
Each employee in an office is assigned a separate, locking cash drawer and is solely responsible for its contents. Cash drawers are to be kept locked whenever out of the employee's line of sight. Under no circumstances is an employee to work from another employee's cash drawer.
Each employee is responsible for his or her own key. If the loss of a cash drawer key necessitates a lock change, the employee may be held responsible for the expense.
Spare keys for the cash drawer, night drop, and office front door must be kept in a sealed, signed, and dated envelope in a secured filing cabinet in the 'A-Z' Files. These envelopes may be opened only on instruction of the Division Manager, with at least two employees present.
Since employees are responsible for the contents of their cash drawers, all software passwords must be kept confidential. There is never a reason to conduct transactions under another employee's login; passwords should be changed whenever an employee feels it necessary.

### 02.05 Transferring Cash Between Drawers
If one employee becomes low on funds, it may be necessary to transfer money between cash drawers. Both employees must count the money being transferred to ensure the correct amount changes hands. In Bravo, select "Transfer Tender," choose the "Source" drawer and the "Destination" drawer, select the bills/amount, and click "save."

### 02.06 Skimming Cash Drawers
Cash drawers are to be skimmed throughout the business day. At no time should a single drawer hold more than $2,000.00. Excess funds should be stored in a secured location elsewhere in the office and/or deposited.

### 02.07 Balancing Cash Drawers
Cash drawers are to be balanced a minimum of two times per day. A receipt tape should be run for each balance; each employee must initial, date, and time-stamp the tape, and these tapes must be filed each day with the end-of-day paperwork.

The final drawer balance is conducted at the end of the day: select "Close Till" from the menu, then "End of Day," and confirm the correct date is entered. If the drawer is over or short, Bravo will alert the user. ### 02.08 Overages / Shortages
If a cash drawer is out of balance, the responsible employee must first recount the drawer to confirm the ending balance was entered correctly, then research the discrepancy. Common causes include:
- Overages: payment not posted; deposit entered twice; cash transfer entered incorrectly; deposit entered incorrectly; wrong account paid out; checks not deposited in system.
- Shortages: payment posted twice; deposit not entered; cash transfer entered incorrectly; deposit entered incorrectly; not enough cash collected; too much cash given back.
If the discrepancy cannot be located, contact the Division Manager immediately; end-of-day reports cannot be run until the Division Manager approves. The overage/shortage must be thoroughly documented in both Bravo and the EOD Form on the company intranet, including: documentation that the Division Manager was contacted; documentation of the scenario causing the discrepancy; the instruction given by the Division Manager; and the date the overage/shortage will be reconciled.
To pull an over/short report: select "Reports" from the home screen, select the "End of Day" report, confirm the date range, and click "Ok."

### 02.09 Interim Deposits / Cash Disbursements
Items needed in the daily course of business are to be paid out through cash disbursements. Purchases of candy, chips, beverages, or any other personal-reason purchase are not acceptable.
To conduct a cash disbursement in Bravo: select the "Tender Transfer" option, select the "source" drawer, the "type" of disbursement, and the amount, with a detailed description; click "save" to finish.
Each cash disbursement must be printed, and the employee conducting it must sign the cash disbursement journal. An itemized receipt for the exact amount and correct date must be attached to the journal. Office supplies are ordered monthly through Home Office and are not normally paid via cash disbursement; an emergency office-supply purchase via cash disbursement requires documented Division Manager approval.
All cash disbursement journals, with itemized receipts and necessary signatures, must be filed in the 'A-Z File,' with a separate file created each month. The branch manager must review the week's cash disbursements weekly and verify all journals/receipts are accounted for.

### 02.13 Daily Open and Close Procedure

#### Opening the Store
- Log into Bravo.
- Select Open Store.
- Select Open Till.
- Select an open amount of $0.00 and click Save.
- Select Transfer Tender.
- On the transfer tender screen, select BANK Account as Source and SAFE Store Safe as Destination. Enter 3000.00 on the Cash heading and click Save (transfers $3,000 from the bank to the store safe).
- Return to the Transfer Tender screen; select SAFE Store Safe as Source and TILL as Destination. Enter 3000.00 on the Cash heading and click Save (transfers $3,000 from the safe to the cash drawer). The store is now ready to conduct transactions.

#### Closing the Store
- Select Close Till.
- Count the cash drawer; enter all funds (cash, credit card, money orders, etc.) on the close screen, with cash entered correctly by denomination and credit-card transactions entered in the correct card-brand column (Visa, Mastercard, etc.). Click Next.
- Verify the entered numbers are correct (use Back to fix errors), then click Save.
- Count total funds, including the amount transferred from the drawer to the store safe and all funds already in the safe, entered correctly by denomination. Click Save, then Close Store.
- Enter total funds again (all cash, credit card, and other monetary instruments, including funds transferred from the cash drawer and those in the store safe). Click Next.
- Verify the entered numbers are correct (use Back to fix errors), then click Save. The store is now closed.
If an overage or shortage is displayed during the count, immediately contact your Division Manager or the Chief Operating Officer to discuss the error. No end-of-day process is complete with an unresolved cash error; this must be resolved with a supervisor prior to leaving for the day.

- Daily Pawn Walks — store managers complete a Daily Pawn Walk each day, verifying and recording all purchases and pawns for that day.
- Transfer Log — staff keep an inventory of transfers out of the store.
- Firearm Transactions (4473) Log — used to track all firearm transactions and the 4473 forms customers complete upon purchase.
- Multiple Gun Sales (3310.4) Log — used when a customer purchases two or more firearms within a five-day period.
- Collections Log — an electronic (Microsoft Excel) log used to track collection efforts for title-pawn customers, including vehicle, loan amount, payment amount, customer name, references, and history of collection efforts.

## Section 4: Screening & Qualifying

### 04.01 New Customers
Properly greeting and interacting with potential customers is a critical responsibility. As a rule of thumb, no customer should get six feet in the door without being greeted (the "Six Feet in the Door" rule). If already working with a customer, let the new arrival know someone will be with them in a moment.
If, during the greeting, an employee notices a customer is pawning or selling a firearm, begin moving from behind the workstation to start screening the item as soon as possible (see Section 04.02, Firearms).
The purpose of screening a customer is to determine whether they wish to pawn or sell their item, to confirm the item works properly, and to confirm it is in fact their item. Each customer must demonstrate how the item works; if the customer is unsure how it works or it does not work properly, this may indicate an attempt to pawn broken material or property belonging to someone else.
Each customer should be asked how much money they are looking for. If the item does not qualify for the amount requested, staff should suggest other items to help the customer reach the amount they need. For buys, explain the purchase contract before writing it up and placing the item in the store's storage area.

### 04.02 Firearms
Firearms are a dangerous item and must be handled carefully, professionally, and properly. Under no circumstances should an employee or customer fire live ammunition. Firearms begin to be screened during the "Six Feet in the Door" greeting: if a customer is looking to pawn or sell a firearm, meet the customer away from behind the workstation and immediately ask whether the firearm is loaded.
For everyone's safety, every firearm should be examined and cleared of ammunition from behind the workstation, with the barrel always facing toward the floor. Disarming procedure varies by firearm type — if in doubt, ask another employee or call your Division Manager immediately.

#### Do's and Don'ts for Handling Firearms
- DO: Greet the customer within the "Six Feet in the Door" area.
- DO: Point the firearm at the ground when inspecting and screening.
- DO: Confirm there is no ammunition loaded and no round in the chamber.
- DO: Ask a fellow employee or contact your Division Manager if you have any questions.
- DO NOT: Fire live ammunition.
- DO NOT: Look down the barrel of the firearm.
- DO NOT: Inspect the firearm from anywhere but behind the workstation.
- DO NOT: Allow the customer to show or disarm the firearm themselves.
Qualifying steps: (1) ask the customer to remove the clip/breach if not already done, at the workstation, pointed in a safe direction; (2) determine whether the customer is pawning or selling (see Section 04.01); (3) determine the condition of the firearm, inspecting it and any accessories thoroughly. All customers must complete a 4473 form for the ATF before the firearm is released back to the customer.

### 04.03 Gold & Jewelry
Each item should be inspected diligently and professionally to prevent purchases of fake or overqualified items. Begin by cleaning the piece with an ultrasonic brush (soft bristles), then wipe and dry it for thorough inspection.

Examine the jewelry with a jeweler's loupe, looking for scratches or file marks that could indicate the item has been pawned or an attempted sale before. Have a second employee examine and gauge the piece to confirm your assessment.
Diamond quality is assessed as follows: Fair Quality (blemishes, small inclusions, somewhat unclean — all Buys are evaluated as fair quality to maximize the store's resale profit; the same stone would be evaluated differently if pawned); Good Quality (very small inclusions, otherwise clear); Great Quality (must be flawless and clear to pawn at this level).

### 04.04 Vehicles

### Precious-Metal Jewelry Listings — Designer / Diamond / Gemstone Only
Effective: July 7, 2026. Applies to all 5 stores — anyone who creates or manages eBay listings. Owner: Joshua Davis / Preston Peters.
Policy: the only precious-metal jewelry that goes on eBay is designer/branded, diamond, or gemstone pieces. Plain gold and silver — generic rings, chains, bracelets, earrings, and wedding bands sold by weight — is not listed on eBay; it stays in the in-store display case.

Why: trailing-12-month sales data shows plain gold and silver essentially does not sell online for Valley Pawn — it sells at the counter. The jewelry that actually moves on eBay is designer/branded and higher-ticket diamond/gemstone pieces reaching eBay's national audience. Listing plain metal online (1) dragged down eBay sell-through metrics with dead inventory, and (2) created double-sale risk (the same piece potentially selling online and in-store at once).
Procedure: at intake/listing, apply the rule above. Selling a designer/diamond piece in-store: Bravo automatically ends the corresponding eBay listing when the counter sale posts (verified 7/7/2026) — no manual delist needed. Delisting must always be done through Bravo to keep the eBay integration in sync.
Background: on July 7, 2026, 87 plain gold/silver listings were delisted from eBay (Roanoke 76, Lexington 7, Culpeper 4); those items returned to sellable in-store stock in Bravo and store managers were notified to re-case them. Designer, diamond, and gemstone pieces were kept listed.

### eBay 30/60/90 Listing-Age Standard
Effective: July 4, 2026. Applies to everyone who creates or manages eBay listings. Owner: Joshua Davis.
Why: eBay is a buy-it-now marketplace — fresh listings sell, stale ones get buried in search. Company sales data (382 sales across all 5 stores) shows the median item sells in 6 days, about 80% within 30 days, and 94% within 90 days. Only 6% of sales ever come from listings older than 90 days, yet 41% of active listings are already past 90 days, tying up value and dragging down ranking and sell-through.
The standard:
- Days 0-14: leave it (freshness window; 64% of sales happen here).
- ~Day 30, no sale: reprice — send an Offer to Watchers, or cut about 10%.
- Day 60: reduce again, or end and relist fresh for a new freshness boost.
- Day 90: pull or final relist. Nothing sits past 90 days as-is.
In one line: reprice at 30, reduce/relist at 60, pull or final-relist at 90.
Support: a weekly report in #ebay-performance flags each store's listings due to reprice (30-59 days), relist/reduce (60-89 days), and pull (90+ days), giving a specific worklist rather than a pile to sort through.
Action required: the formal one-page policy is sent to each employee in Gusto for review and e-signature.

## Section 7: Marketing

### 07.01 General Practices
If a potential customer takes the time to contact our office, assume they will also take the time to contact competitors — the phone call is often the customer's first impression of the business. The telephone should always be answered professionally: clearly, with enthusiasm, and without mumbling.

### 07.02 Customer Demographics
Telemarketing and outreach efforts are focused on the store's own customer base (see Section 07.06), particularly inactive customers, in line with do-not-call compliance considerations.

### 07.03 Telephone Etiquette
- Answer within the first two rings whenever possible.
- Include all products in the greeting; the customer may not know everything the store offers.
- Be positive, professional, and courteous; speak clearly and enunciate.
- Ask the potential customer's name and use it appropriately throughout the call.
- Be as knowledgeable as possible about all products; if you need assistance, advise the caller before placing them on hold, and do not leave them on hold for prolonged periods.
- Thank the customer for holding when you return to the call.
- Offer to take the customer's application information over the phone once questions are answered.

### 07.04 Customer Service
The most important marketing activity is providing outstanding customer service. Great service drives retention and referrals; indifferent service — more than rudeness — is the leading reason customers stop returning (per a 1997 American Society for Quality Control survey, cited in the legacy manual).
First impressions form at the first point of contact — the telephone and the first branch visit. Storefronts should always be clean, neat, and professional: signage properly displayed and maintained, floors vacuumed/swept, windows and partitions clean, displays neat (no handwritten signs), countertops clutter-free, and all lights working.
Greeting standards: dress neatly and professionally, wear a name tag, acknowledge every customer entering ("Welcome to Valley Pawn!"), walk around the counter to greet them, shake hands, introduce yourself, offer a business card, and ask how you can help.
When presenting products and services, explain that the company offers both selling an item outright and pawn loans using the item as collateral; explain how a pawn loan works, and highlight benefits such as confidentiality, competitive pricing, other available products (including check cashing), the paid referral program, and membership in CFSA (an industry trade association advocating for high ethical standards and customer credit access).
When handling objections (e.g., "I want to think about it," "I can get cheaper rates elsewhere," "I'm just curious what you do here"), stay positive, answer questions fully, and always get Division/Region Manager approval before offering a lower rate than standard. General tips: be confident, give the customer your undivided attention, don't rush, and let genuine enthusiasm show.

### 07.05 Referral Program
Referrals are one of the most important new-customer channels (30% of new customers in 2009 came from referrals, per the legacy manual, second only to store signage) and are effectively free marketing. The foundation of a strong referral program is excellent customer service.
Referral cards are given to every customer, who writes the referring customer's name on the back; the referral fee is sent to the referring customer once the new customer's business is completed. Referrals should also be requested during telemarketing and weekly courtesy calls. Personal referrals are not exempt from do-not-call registries, so employees should not follow up on them by calling. Employees and relatives of Valley Pawn are not eligible to receive referral payments. Referral processing is centralized through Home Office.

### 07.06 Telemarketing
Telemarketing keeps the company in contact with customers between visits, focused on the store's own (especially inactive) customers due to do-not-call restrictions. It must be scheduled and tracked as a core, recurring activity — at least three days per week, one hour per day — logged on the Store Telemarketing Log and reviewed weekly by the branch manager and periodically by the Division Manager.
Call preparation follows five steps: have the Telemarketing Manual open to the right script; review the prior week's Telemarketing Log for needed follow-up; review the customer's file/status/history; dial, using the script as a guide while practicing proper etiquette; and log the call. Representative script categories in the legacy manual include: 15-45 day inactive check-ins, 45+ day inactive win-backs, new-branch-manager introductions, $10 discount offers, and business-partner outreach — always following proper telephone etiquette and getting Division Manager approval before offering discounted rates.
Objection-handling tips mirror those in Section 07.04 — never bash competitors; instead emphasize the value of Valley Pawn's service, and try to match competitive rates only with manager approval.

### 07.07 Business Partnerships
A business partnership is a mutually beneficial relationship between a branch and another local business, most successfully built around the check-cashing product. Good partner candidates include businesses that generate a lot of checks or employ people likely to need check-cashing services — for example: auto parts suppliers, car washes, discount hair salons, discount retailers, family/fast-food restaurants, grocery stores, healthcare facilities, insurance companies, lenders that do not offer check cashing, mobile home parks, personal injury attorneys, rent-to-own centers, retirement communities, tax preparers, and thrift shops.
To establish a partnership: identify the right decision-maker; approach by phone, appointment, or introductory letter; follow up with a phone call or visit; and send thank-you notes after both the meeting and any partnership agreement. Internally: provide the partner with marked flyers or a stamp; log each new referred customer in a Business Referral Log; have the customer complete a Referral Authorization Form; fax the form to Home Office for verification and payment by mail.

### 07.08 Customer Appreciation Days
Customer Appreciation Days reward current customers, re-engage inactive accounts, market to new customers, and strengthen business-partner relationships. Four company-wide mandatory events occur annually: Easter, Independence Day, Labor Day/Back to School, and Thanksgiving/Black Friday (launch of retail season). Unless otherwise authorized, spending should not exceed $25 per event — keep costs down through partner co-hosting, donations, and community-organization participation.
Planning timeline: 6-8 weeks ahead, decide the event type/prize/permits and line up business and community partners; 4 weeks ahead, develop flyers and other collateral with the Division Manager and Marketing Director, and begin distributing flyers; 3 weeks ahead, call inactive customers; 2 weeks ahead, confirm partner/donor participation; the week of, pick up donations and prepare supplies; day of, execute the event; the week after, send thank-you notes to all partners and participating organizations.
Prize drawings must always be open to anyone who walks in the door (no purchase, loan, payment, or transaction may be required to enter), though additional entries may be given for subsequent visits or for donating goods to a food/toy drive. Use entry forms as an opportunity to update customer records in Bravo.

## Section 8: Collections

### 08.01 Pawn Collection Calls
Pawn Courtesy Calls remind customers that their pawn is due. These calls begin seven days before the due date and continue until either the due date or customer contact is made. Company policy limits contact to one call per day, and no message is left on the customer's voicemail or answering machine.

### 08.02 FDCPA
The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA), a federal law enacted in 1977 in response to widespread abusive, deceptive, and unfair debt collection practices, guides all Valley Pawn collection procedures. Following the FDCPA protects both debtors and the company.

#### Hours & Frequency of Contact
Debtors may only be contacted Monday through Saturday, and not before 8:00 AM or after 9:00 PM, unless the customer requests otherwise. Do not contact a debtor (or any reference) with a frequency or at hours that could reasonably be interpreted as threatening or harassing. Any reference who requests no further contact must be noted in the comment screen, and all contact with that reference must cease.

#### Privacy & Confidentiality
Do not discuss a debtor's account in front of anyone other than the debtor and their spouse. When mailing collection letters, do not indicate on the envelope that a debt is owed — use the plain office stamp (address only, no company name/phone number).

#### Disinterested Third Parties & Employers
Anyone other than the debtor, the debtor's spouse, or the debtor's attorney is a disinterested third party — never divulge account details or request payment from such a party. Do not communicate or threaten to communicate with a debtor's employer in a way that discloses the debt; honor any request (from the debtor or employer) for no workplace calls. Routine employment verification is not prohibited.

#### Prohibited Conduct
- Harassment — profane/obscene language, anonymous or collect calls, cash-on-delivery letters, repeated ringing believed to avoid the debtor, or any activity that abuses, ridicules, or degrades the consumer, or calls to the workplace against instruction.
- Impersonation — a collector must always identify with true company and personal name; no aliases, code names, or impersonation of any other person or agency.
- False, misleading, or deceptive tactics — regarding who you are, why you are calling, the status of the debt, or the action you intend to take.
- Threats — no statements threatening force, violence, criminal prosecution, or that legal action will cause the debtor to defend themselves in criminal court, or that the debtor's status will be disclosed to friends/relatives/employer/government.
- Trickery — offering references something of value for information, leaving misleading messages, vague claims of being a collection agency/credit bureau/attorney, or printed materials that could be mistaken for a summons, warrant, telegram, or check.

### 08.03 Past Due Loans - Pull Process
A past-due loan is one or more days late based on the due date. All loans receive a reminder call no less than two days before the due date; calls are logged in Bravo's contacted section only when direct contact is made, with any commitment details noted.
If a customer states they will not redeem or pay, this is entered in the notes and the item is tagged for pull on the date allowed by state regulation. If the customer commits to pay, the item may be held up to 30 days after the due date depending on the commitment (multiple commitments allowed, capped at 30 days from the original due date, each logged in Bravo notes). If no contact is made, the item is held for 30 days with a daily contact attempt; on the 30th day without contact, it must be pulled and tagged for sale.
No exceptions to this policy without written Division Manager approval, based on the customer's payment history or ability to pay, at the Division Manager's discretion. Every effort must be made to inform the customer of the consequences of non-payment and the company's right to sell pawned items on default; upon default, the item becomes the sole property of Valley Pawn.

## Section 10: Safety & Security

### 10.01 Safety
Office security protects employees, customers, and the company's interests and assets. The first rule of security is to always be observant. Rear/side doors must be secured at all times; when possible, two employees should travel to the bank together and call for a police escort if anyone suspicious is noticed; conceal the bank bag and money in transit (e.g., inside a large folder or shopping bag). All non-employees are restricted to the lobby area — never allow a non-employee behind the counter.

#### Safety in the Workplace
All employees are expected to be safety-conscious and to report any unsafe condition, regardless of severity, to the Division Manager. A clean work area is a safer one. Tips: open only one file drawer at a time; keep the lobby, work areas, and exits clear of obstacles; secure wires/cords/supplies/boxes; keep office chairs well maintained; never attempt repairs requiring special tools, and never use furniture as a makeshift ladder or tool; contact the Division Manager for any necessary equipment/facility repair; watch for sharp-object hazards; and keep the parking area clear of objects that could cause injury.

#### Reporting Injuries
Contact the Human Resources Department and Division Manager immediately if an injury occurs; complete and submit an injury report (available from HR) right away. The injured employee must obtain a doctor's release or written HR permission before returning to work.

### 10.02 Store Locks & Keys
Division Manager approval is required before issuing keys to any employee; no one employed less than 90 days is issued keys unless the Division Manager specifies otherwise. Office locks are re-keyed or replaced any time a keyholding employee is terminated or resigns — the Division Manager can do this without a locksmith. The Branch Manager and Division Manager are jointly responsible for compliance with this policy.

### 10.03 Severe Weather Evacuation
In the event of a mandatory hurricane, flood, or tornado evacuation, the following procedure secures the office and employees:
- Contact your Division Manager for approval to proceed, providing your pertinent contact information (cell phone, personal email).
- Tape all office windows in an 'X' pattern to prevent shattered glass.
- Run the End of Day report and prepare the deposit (offices with a petty cash fund prepare a separate deposit for those funds).
- Place all computer equipment in large plastic trash bags and raise it off the floor.
- Place all customer checks in a locked filing cabinet.
- Gather telephone contact numbers for all branch employees, and make sure everyone has the Division Manager, Region Manager, and Home Office numbers stored in their phones.
- Contact your Division Manager to confirm the procedure is complete before leaving.
- Lock all doors securing the branch for departure and deposit all funds at the bank.
- Remain in daily contact with your Division Manager for assistance and instruction; if unreachable, follow the chain of command.

### 10.04 Burglaries & Theft Procedures

#### Principles and Standards
Personal safety — yours and the customer's — is paramount if a robbery occurs. Do not take physical or heroic action. Personal firearms are not permitted inside the store and should never be used in the event of a robbery. Always cooperate fully so the robbery ends quickly. Lock the doors afterward, do not allow anyone in, and do not disturb anything. Recall events and descriptions with all parties present and document immediately. Do not discuss the robbery or the loss amount with anyone other than police and upper management.

#### Robbery Prevention
- Be aware of traffic and pedestrians on the property; observe customer patterns, visitors, or unusual activity.
- Greet everyone who enters — customers appreciate it, robbers don't.
- Skim the drawer frequently per company policy; the cash drawer must be locked when unattended.
- Never count money or skim the drawer in view of customers or passers-by.
- Never leave the branch or front counter unattended.
- Lock the front door before beginning end-of-day balancing and deposit procedures.
- Make daily deposits and go to the bank when cash on hand exceeds $3,000.

#### During a Robbery
An armed robber is a desperate and irrational person: listen and obey exact instructions; cooperate fully and never create conflict with the robber's requests; concentrate on and observe the robber and weapon to recall as much detail as possible; ask customers/witnesses to help document descriptions and, if possible, to wait for police (if they cannot wait, get contact information for the authorities to follow up); complete and mail an Incident Report to Home Office within 72 hours.

### 10.05 Burglary and After-Hours Emergency

#### After the Robbery
Call 911; contact both your Division and Region Managers, who will inform Home Office; lock all doors until police arrive and allow no one in; place a 'closed' sign on the door; do not disturb anything (the police may need to dust for fingerprints).

#### Observation Items
Recall as accurately as possible: height and weight; approximate age; hair and eye color; build; speech defect or accent; type/color of clothing; marks, scars, or deformities; a written or drawn description of the weapon (size, color, automatic or revolver if a handgun); which hand held the weapon; and the direction the robber was last seen headed. Conduct a cash drawer audit and report any missing money to the Division Manager.

#### Media/Press and Reporting
Refer all media/press questions to Home Office — do not discuss any information with media under any circumstances. Report any incident by phone immediately to your Division and Region Managers, and complete an Incident Report listing any missing money, checks, or equipment; fax and mail it to Home Office within 72 hours. Compliance with these procedures ensures proper insurance claims handling.

### 10.06 Firearm Handling and Safety
No handgun or long gun should ever be loaded with ammunition at any time; customers are never permitted to load a weapon during a transaction or while handling it. All firearms brought in by customers should be unloaded with any clips/magazines removed. Employees and customers are prohibited from dry-firing any firearm. A customer requesting to handle a firearm should never be given it with the clip/magazine inserted — the clip is retained by the employee and given to the customer only if the weapon is purchased.

## Section 11: Glossary

### 11.01 Glossary of Terms
- Buy Transaction: A transaction in which the customer's item is bought, rather than used as collateral for a pawn loan.
- Finance Fee: The amount of funds charged to the customer for loaning money on their item; the cost of borrowing.
- Grace Period: The amount of time between when a customer becomes delinquent on a pawn and when the item is merchandised and made ready to sell.
- Gun Lock: An important tool used to prevent a firearm from being discharged while the device is in place.
- Karat: The measure of the purity of gold.
- Layaway: A customer pays a deposit to secure an item for later purchase.
- Merchandising: Prepping items to be sold via storefront or internet mediums.
- Pawn: A transaction in which a customer puts up an item as collateral to borrow money.
- Pawn Ticket: The customer's contract and proof of pawn, used when redeeming or refinancing an item.
- Profit: Total amount of fees earned minus any outstanding expenses.
- Qualifying: The process of determining an item's value and what a customer wants to do with it (pawn or sell).
- Redemption: A transaction in which a customer pays the loan amount plus finance fees to reclaim their pawned item.
- Refinance: Taking out a new pawn loan by paying the current finance charges on an existing loan, without redeeming the item outright.
- PFI (Pull for Inventory): The process of pulling defaulted pawn collateral from loan status into sellable store inventory (see Section 06.01).
- NCO / NCOM: Neat, Clean, and Orderly (later expanded internally to include Merchandised, and further to Professional) — the storefront-standards checklist referenced in Section 03.16.
- MHG: Multiple Handgun (Sales) — see the Multiple Handgun Sales Protocol in Sections 04.02 and 10.06.
- 4473 / 3310.4: ATF firearm transaction forms — see Sections 04.02 and 05.02.
- FFL: Federal Firearms License — Valley Pawn holds an FFL at the Roanoke location.
- FDCPA: Fair Debt Collection Practices Act — see Section 08.02.

### Harassment/Workplace Violence and Disciplinary Action
The company intends to provide a work environment that is pleasant, professional, and free from intimidation, hostility or other offenses, which might interfere, or have the potential to interfere, with an employee’s work performance. Harassment of any kind, whether verbal, physical, visual or otherwise, will not be tolerated. ### Harassment
Harassment in the workplace can take many forms. It may be in the form of, but not limited to, words, offensive jokes, pranks, intimidation, physical assaults, physical contact, or violence. It can also be in the form of written or printed material such as notes, photographs, signs, cartoons, posters, Internet websites, email jokes or statements, and articles of a harassing and/or offensive nature. Harassment is not necessarily sexual in nature. A statement or action regarding a co-worker that is considered offensive, derogatory, violent, or intimidating, whether directed at them or not, may be considered a form of harassment.

### Sexual Harassment
Generally, two categories of sexual harassment exist, however sexual harassment can be expressed in many forms.
The first category may be defined as an exchange of, or a request for sexual favors for improvement in another employee's working conditions and/or compensation. It also encompasses such conduct when it is expressed as a term or condition of employment and/or compensation. This includes either implicitly or explicitly, and when an employment decision is based on an individual's acceptance or rejection of such conduct.
The second category is defined as a hostile, intimidating, or offensive working environment. This category includes but is not limited to, unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, sexual jokes, sexual oriented comments, verbal or physical contact of a sexual nature when such conduct creates an offensive, hostile and/or intimidating working environment. Examples of an offensive, hostile, and/or intimidating working environment includes, but is not limited to, pictures, cartoons, symbols, emails, internet websites, or any other items or materials found to be offensive, and/or sexual in nature or content. It is against company policy for any employee to view and/or download inappropriate pictures or materials onto the company computer systems.

### Reporting Harassment
The company prohibits any employee from retaliating in any way against anyone who has raised any concern about harassment of any kind. The company will fully investigate any complaint of harassment and will take prompt and appropriate disciplinary action if it is reasonably determined that harassment of any kind has taken place. It is essential that the employee notify the supervisor, Human Resource Department, or other member of upper management with whom the employee is comfortable speaking to immediately, even if the employee is unsure if the behavior is considered harassment.
It is the supervisor's responsibility to report any such incidences of harassment to upper management, and the Human Resource Department immediately. All reports will be promptly investigated with due regard to the privacy of everyone involved. However, confidentiality cannot be guaranteed. Any employee(s) found to have harassed another employee would be subject to appropriate disciplinary action up to and including termination. The company will also take any
additional action deemed necessary to appropriately remedy the situation. Retaliation of any sort will not be permitted. No adverse employment action will be taken against an employee making a good faith report of alleged harassment.
Each employee has the responsibility to help keep his or her work environment free from any kind of harassment. Any employee, who becomes aware of or suspects an incident of harassment, whether by witnessing the incident or being told of it, must report the incident to their supervisor, Human Resource Department, or someone in upper management with whom they would feel more comfortable. Once management is aware of the harassment complaint the company has an obligation to take prompt and appropriate action, regardless of whether or not the person reporting it desires the company to do so.
All harassment and discriminatory policies apply to all areas of employment, including recruitment, hiring, training, development, promotion, transfer, termination, layoff, compensation benefits, and all other conditions and privileges of employment in accordance with the applicable federal, state, and local laws.

### Social Security
The U.S. government operates a system of mandated insurance known as social security. Wage earners are required by law to contribute a set amount of their weekly wages to the trust fund from which benefits are paid. Reasonable suspicion testing: The Company may require employees to submit to a drug and/or alcohol test in accordance with applicable federal, state and local laws when there is reasonable suspicion to believe that the employee may be using or under the influence of illegal drugs and/or alcohol, or is otherwise in violation of this policy. Therefore, drugs, alcohol, or paraphernalia possibly used in connection with illicit drugs found on the employee’s person or at or near the employee’s work area may trigger a request for a reasonable suspicion test.
Reasonable suspicion testing will generally not be conducted for employees, except as permitted by applicable federal and state laws.

### Cannabis
Consistent with Virginia law, employees may not possess or use cannabis on Company premises or during work hours, and may not be impaired by cannabis while performing Company business. The Company may take action, including discipline, based on a positive test or evidence of impairment, regardless of whether use occurred on or off duty. Employees who are registered medical cannabis patients under Virginia law and who use cannabis oil off-duty and in compliance with their written certification will not be disciplined solely for that lawful off-duty use, provided they are not impaired at work and do not possess cannabis on Company property.

### Disciplinary Warnings
All employment with the company is at-will. Any employee, including an employee whose conduct is unacceptable or who does not display satisfactory job performance and/or accomplishments may be dismissed without any prior disciplinary action. An employee may be given either a verbal or a written warning that continued misconduct or failure to correct performance deficiencies could result in discharge.

### Verbal Warning
The employee's supervisor may discuss and/or explain the problem or violation
and communicate the expected remedy. The purpose of the warning is to remind the employee what the rule or performance expectation is, and also to remind the employee of his or her responsibility to meet the company's standards of conduct and/or performance. The supervisor will document the warning and a copy will be placed in the employee's personnel file.

### Written Warning/Mutual Action Plan
A written warning may be issued for violation of company policies, procedures, rules, or standards of conduct. The supervisor will discuss the seriousness of the situation and will also discuss the employee's expected performance and/or behavior. The supervisor will document the warning and a copy will be placed in the employee's personnel file.

### Reprimand/Record of Warning
A reprimand may be issued for repeated and/or new violation of company policy, procedures, rules, or standards of conduct. The employee's supervisor will discuss the seriousness of the situation and will also discuss the expected performance and/or behavior. The supervisor will document the reprimand and a copy will be placed in the employee's personnel file.

### Suspension
If an employee commits an action listed below, or any other action not specified but determined by the company to be similarly serious, he or she may be terminated or, at the company's discretion, suspended without pay pending an investigation of the situation.
Following any investigation, the employee may be terminated without prior or additional disciplinary action.
- Theft
- Falsification of company records
- Failure to follow safety practices and procedures
- Breach of confidentiality policy and procedures
- Threat of, or the act of doing bodily harm
- Willful or negligent destruction of property
- Use and/or possession of intoxicants, drugs, or narcotics
- Deliberate and direct insubordination toward a supervisor
- Harassment of any kind

### Termination
The provisions of the company's various disciplinary actions are not a guarantee of the procedures being implemented. The company reserves the right to terminate employment at any time, with or without reason. Additionally, the company reserves the right to assist the District Attorney's Office to prosecute any employee for the alleged breach of any law. An employee may be dismissed for failure to perform assigned tasks satisfactorily, or for conduct detrimental to the company or its operations, or for no reason at all. An employee may be dismissed for the violation of any policy or procedure in this handbook, the company Policy and Procedure Manual, or for the violation of any other company policies or procedures whether or not set forth in these documents.
As stated previously, the company operates under the principle of at-will employment. Neither the employee nor the company has entered into a contract regarding the duration of employment. The employee is free to
terminate employment at any time, with or without reason. Likewise, the company is free to terminate an employee's employment at any time, with or without reason, at its sole discretion. However, the company would hope and expect a two-week notice to be given in the event of an employee's resignation. Any vacation time earned as of the termination date will be paid to the employee on the next regularly scheduled pay date. Any earned sick days are forfeited upon termination.

### Exit Interviews
Employees will be granted the opportunity to have an exit interview to allow the resigned employee an opportunity to disclose the reasons for his or her leaving and to communicate the employee's impression of the company. The main purpose of the exit interview is to be certain that a misunderstanding or erroneous situation has not occurred. Also, the exit interview may give the company insight into areas of opportunity of improvement.
Post-Employment Inquiries The company does not respond to verbal requests for an employee reference. The Human Resource Department may be
able to provide a reference to potential employers only if the employee has completed and signed a release form.
An employee of the company may not under any circumstances respond to any requests for information regarding another employee, a former employee or a job applicant unless it is part of his or her assigned job responsibilities. Such requests must be forwarded to the Human Resource Department.

### Return of Company Property
Any company property issued to an employee, including but not limited to such items as vehicle, laptop computer, office keys, credit cards, business cards, cell phones, etc. must be returned to the company at the company's request or the time of termination. The employee is responsible for any lost or damaged items. The value of the property that was issued but not returned may be deducted from the employee's paycheck. For this purpose, the employee may be asked to sign a wage deduction authorization form.
