## Section 1: Employee Administration

### 01.01 Hours of Operation
STORE HOURS. Culpeper: Monday through Saturday, 10:00 AM to 6:00 PM. Harrisonburg, Waynesboro, Lexington and Roanoke: Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday and Saturday, 10:00 AM to 6:00 PM — CLOSED WEDNESDAY. All five stores are closed Sunday. No Valley Pawn store ever closes at 5:00 PM; Culpeper is the only store open on Wednesday. All personnel must be in the store preparing to open no later than 9:30 AM, and remain until all customers have been served and all daily duties are completed.
Scheduled office-personnel hours should coincide with the activities and daily volume of the office (for example, it is better to schedule a doctor's appointment for a slower day such as Tuesday rather than a high-volume day such as Monday or Friday).
Employees are required to take a thirty (30) minute lunch break each day. Where more than one employee is on duty, lunch breaks must be staggered so the store remains open, and the employee is fully relieved of duty for the full thirty minutes — that break is unpaid.
SOLO COVERAGE — THE LUNCH BREAK IS PAID. Where only one employee is on duty, that employee remains responsible for the store, the safe, the firearms and the inventory throughout the lunch period and is therefore NOT relieved of duty. That thirty minutes is PAID TIME WORKED — do not clock out for it. The employee eats at the store and remains available to customers; the front door stays open during published hours unless the Market Manager has authorised a posted closure window for that store on solo-coverage days. Where coverage allows an employee to be genuinely relieved and to leave the premises, the break is unpaid in the normal way.
ALL BREAKS ARE COMPLETED BY 2:00 PM (or by 3:00 PM if coordinated in advance with Preston Peters), so that the store is fully staffed through the afternoon peak. Use the break button in the timekeeping app for every break.
Prior Market Manager approval is required if an employee needs to be out of the office for longer than one hour. If an office is not meeting company standards for growth and/or delinquency, the Market Manager may schedule additional night and weekend hours. No employee may work overtime without prior Market Manager approval.

Scheduling follows the hours-of-operation and staffing framework in Sections 01.01 and 01.03 above.

Each pawnshop is staffed with one full-time Manager and a Pawnbroker, allowing the store to focus on different parts of the business (collections, marketing, etc.) without disruption. Employee lunch breaks must be staggered so the store stays open at all times.

### 01.04 Time Sheet Policy
Clock in and out using the GEOFENCE in the timekeeping app, at the store, every time — never from any other location, unless attending a meeting or covering a store outside your normal commute. Use the BREAK BUTTON for every break. If the geofence fails, call Preston Peters (Market Manager) immediately so the punch can be recorded — do not wait until the end of the day. Be honest and accurate with time records.
Timecards must accurately reflect all time actually worked. Where a timecard needs correction (for example a missed clock-in or clock-out), the correction is applied to the CURRENT payroll whenever it reaches payroll before that payroll is processed. If a correction is identified after processing has already run, it is paid on the next payroll and no later. This is a processing limitation, not a penalty: the Company does not withhold, delay or forfeit pay for any time actually worked, and no employee is ever docked for a timekeeping error.
If any employee believes they have been underpaid for any reason, they should raise it with Preston Peters or Joshua Davis immediately. The Company will investigate and pay any shortfall within fourteen (14) days of receiving notice. Repeated failure to clock in or out is addressed as a performance matter through the normal corrective-action process, never by adjusting, delaying or withholding pay.
Report a missed punch to Preston Peters (Market Manager) — or, if he cannot be reached, to Joshua Davis (CEO) — as soon as it is noticed. The Company is responsible for paying all time worked whether or not the punch was recorded correctly.

### 01.06 Branch Visitors
Anyone entering the store who is not a customer and not an employee is a visitor. Visitors fall into two groups and they are handled DIFFERENTLY. Group 1 — law enforcement and government inspectors: police and sheriff's officers, ATF, state and federal law-enforcement officials, state examiners, and fire, building and health inspectors. Group 2 — everyone else, including news media, press, salespeople and solicitors. Read the correct group below before doing anything.
GROUP 1 — LAW ENFORCEMENT AND GOVERNMENT INSPECTORS: ADMIT THEM IMMEDIATELY.
Ask to see credentials and record the officer's name, agency and badge or ID number. Verifying identity is the only step that comes before access.
Admit them and cooperate fully and promptly. Virginia law (Va. Code § 54.1-4011) REQUIRES every pawnbroker and every employee to admit any duly authorized law-enforcement officer of the jurisdiction, and any law-enforcement official of the state or federal government, during business hours, and to permit examination of all records required by the pawnbroker statutes and of any article believed to be missing or stolen. As a federal firearms licensee the Company is separately subject to ATF inspection of its firearms records and inventory during business hours.
Do NOT delay an officer or inspector, do not refuse or restrict access to records or inventory, and do not make access conditional on reaching a manager first. Notify Preston Peters (Market Manager) and Joshua Davis (CEO) at the same time you are cooperating — notification happens IN PARALLEL with cooperation, never before it.
If an officer takes possession of any article, the officer must provide a receipt for it (Va. Code § 54.1-4011). Obtain that receipt, keep it with the transaction records, and photograph it.
Be courteous and answer questions about records and inventory truthfully. If asked a question you do not know the answer to, say so and offer to connect them with Preston Peters or Joshua Davis — do not guess.
GROUP 2 — MEDIA, PRESS AND ALL OTHER VISITORS.
If the visitor is a member of the press or media: smile, say 'no comment,' and politely ask them to leave. Refer them to Joshua Davis (CEO) and notify Preston Peters immediately.
Do not allow anyone in this group behind the workstation. Do not allow photographs to be taken, do not allow customers to be interviewed inside the store, and do not discuss any Company matter with a press or media visitor. Violations may result in disciplinary action.
Nothing in this section restricts any employee's right to speak with a government agency, to file a charge or complaint, or to discuss wages, hours or working conditions. See the Protected Rights statement in the front of this manual.

### 01.11 Paid Time Off (PTO) Policy
This policy replaces all prior PTO communications. Effective October 20, 2025.
Purpose: to provide employees with meaningful, uninterrupted time away from work; ensure proper staffing during high-volume business periods; promote fairness and consistency across all locations; and allow management to effectively plan schedules.

#### PTO Eligibility & Accrual
- After 1 year of service: 40 hours annually.
- After 2 or more years of service: 80 hours annually.

#### Required PTO Block Usage
Employees must use a minimum of 40 consecutive hours (one full workweek) of PTO each calendar year.

#### PTO Blackout Periods
PTO requests will not be approved during the following periods:
- November 28 - January 5 (Holiday Season)
- February 1 - March 31 (Tax Refund Season)

#### Requesting PTO
- PTO must be requested at least 30 days in advance.
- All requests must be submitted through Gusto.
- Approval is subject to business needs.

#### Use-It-or-Lose-It Policy
Unused PTO does not roll over and is forfeited at year-end. PTO is never paid out if the employee is not currently working for the company, was terminated, and/or resigned.

#### Exceptions
Exceptions. The blackout restrictions do not apply to any absence protected by law. This includes reasonable accommodation under the Americans with Disabilities Act and the Virginia Human Rights Act, military leave under USERRA, jury duty, voting leave, leave required by Virginia law, and any other legally protected absence. Requests of this kind are not treated as PTO requests and are directed to Preston Peters (Market Manager).
Employees are responsible for understanding and complying with this policy. Questions should be directed to management.

## Section 2: Funds Control

### 02.01 General Policies
Company funds are not to be used for personal or unauthorized purposes. Company funds include petty cash, change fund, customer payments, and check stock. ### 02.02 Safe Policies
The store safe holds the day's operating cash, jewelry inventory and firearms when they are not on the floor. The safe is opened only by the Store Manager or a designated employee, is kept closed and locked between uses, and is never opened in view of a customer. Combinations and keys are never shared outside the Store Manager and the Market Manager.

### 02.03 Bank Withdrawals
Each morning the Store Manager requests any necessary funds from the Company's bank. Before leaving the bank, manually verify the money at the teller's window and confirm the teller has properly validated the withdrawal slip with the correct business date, account number and amount.

### 04.01 New Customers

### 04.02 Firearms
Firearms are a dangerous item and must be handled carefully, professionally, and properly. Under no circumstances should an employee or customer fire live ammunition. Firearms begin to be screened during the "Six Feet in the Door" greeting: if a customer is looking to pawn or sell a firearm, meet the customer away from behind the workstation and immediately ask whether the firearm is loaded.
For everyone's safety, every firearm should be examined and cleared of ammunition from behind the workstation, with the barrel always facing toward the floor. Disarming procedure varies by firearm type — if in doubt, ask another employee or call your Market Manager immediately.

#### Do's and Don'ts for Handling Firearms
- DO: Greet the customer within the "Six Feet in the Door" area.
- DO: Point the firearm at the ground when inspecting and screening.
- DO: Confirm there is no ammunition loaded and no round in the chamber.
- DO: Ask a fellow employee or contact your Market Manager if you have any questions.
- DO NOT: Fire live ammunition.
- DO NOT: Look down the barrel of the firearm.
- DO NOT: Inspect the firearm from anywhere but behind the workstation.
- DO NOT: Allow the customer to show or disarm the firearm themselves.
ONE SEQUENCE, EVERY TIME. (1) Greet the customer away from the counter and ask whether the firearm is loaded. (2) Take control of the firearm from the customer, muzzle pointed at the floor — the customer does not demonstrate, unload or clear it. (3) Behind the workstation, the EMPLOYEE removes the magazine, opens the action and confirms the chamber is empty. (4) Determine whether the customer is pawning or selling (Section 04.01). (5) Inspect the firearm and accessories and determine condition. A customer redeeming a pawned firearm completes Form 4473 and the Virginia State Police check before the firearm is released (Section 05.01).

#### Broken / Non-Functioning Firearm Storage Protocol
- All broken/non-functioning firearms must be logged in Bravo and the bound book identically to functioning firearms — no exceptions.
- Physical storage: non-functioning firearms must be stored separately and labeled "NON-FUNCTIONING — DO NOT SELL AS-IS."

#### Multiple Handgun Sales Protocol
- VIRGINIA ONE-HANDGUN-PER-30-DAYS — THE GATE COMES FIRST. Under Va. Code § 18.2-308.2:2(R) it is unlawful for any person who is not a licensed dealer to purchase more than one handgun in any 30-day period. BEFORE a second handgun is sold to the same customer within 30 days, the store must verify and record one of the statutory exceptions, or REFUSE THE SALE. The exceptions the counter will actually meet are: (a) the customer holds a valid Virginia concealed handgun permit — record the permit number; (b) the customer presents a Virginia State Police multiple-handgun purchase certificate — it is valid for seven days, must be surrendered to the store before the sale and kept on file for at least two years; (c) a trade-in of a handgun as part of the same transaction, limited to one such transaction per day; (d) replacement of a handgun stolen or lost within the last 30 days, with a copy of the police report attached to the transaction record; (e) a law-enforcement officer or agency. 'Purchase' does not include a same-seller exchange or replacement within 30 days. A sale that fails this gate is not made, however it is papered afterwards.
- Once the Virginia gate is satisfied, the FEDERAL reporting steps below apply. Two or more pistols or revolvers sold or transferred to the same unlicensed customer within five consecutive BUSINESS days triggers the ATF multiple-sale report (Form 3310.4), filed no later than close of business on the day of the multiple sale. The Store Manager is notified immediately when a second handgun sale to the same customer is identified.
- The store manager must be notified immediately when a second handgun sale to the same customer is identified.
- The ATF form must be completed and submitted within the required regulatory timeframe.
- Documentation is retained in the store compliance file for a minimum of five years.
- Employees may not structure sales to avoid reporting thresholds — doing so is a federal violation and a terminable offense.
- A Multiple Handgun Sales (MHG) Acknowledgement Form is signed by the customer at the time of the second handgun purchase, documenting their acknowledgement of the reporting requirement. It is retained with the transaction records for a minimum of five years and is in addition to — never a replacement for — the Virginia gate above and ATF Form 3310.4. Customer refusal to sign is noted and the Store Manager notified.

### 04.03 Gold & Jewelry
Each item should be inspected diligently and professionally to prevent purchases of fake or overqualified items. Begin by cleaning the piece with an ultrasonic brush (soft bristles), then wipe and dry it for thorough inspection.
Determine whether the customer is pawning or selling the piece (see Section 04.01) and check whether the item has been in the store before, and if so, what was previously loaned on it.
If the jewelry contains gold, it is often stamped with its karat value; in any case, test the piece to confirm the gold is genuine and determine its karat. Use the magnet test (if the metal attracts, it is not real gold — be careful to test the gold portion, not other parts such as a metal clasp), then perform an acid test by lightly scratching the gold onto a testing slate and applying the appropriate acid.
Examine the jewelry with a jeweler's loupe, looking for scratches or file marks that could indicate the item has been pawned or an attempted sale before. Have a second employee examine and gauge the piece to confirm your assessment.
Diamond quality is assessed as Fair (blemishes, small inclusions, somewhat unclean), Good (very small inclusions, otherwise clear) or Great (flawless and clear). Internal pricing basis: outright buys are priced off the Fair grade to protect resale margin; the same stone pawned may be valued off a higher grade. This is how the store sets its own offer — it is not a statement made to the customer about the stone.

A full Vehicle Inspection Form is completed and signed by both staff and the customer, and filed with the customer's folder along with a copy of the title, utility bill, copy of ID, and the Virginia DMV lien recordation paperwork required under Va. Code § 46.2-637. The inspection should confirm the VIN on the vehicle matches the VIN on the title (typically on the windshield, driver's side), that the vehicle starts and shifts normally, and should assess the interior and exterior (valid, non-expired tags and inspection sticker — never a rejection sticker; visible damage such as peeling paint, rust, pre-existing body damage, mismatched panels, or mismatched/balding tires are red flags requiring Market Manager involvement or likely denial).
Advance limits: the loan amount must not exceed 50% of the vehicle's value as determined from the valuation source designated by the Market Manager, and the standard working target is 40% of that value. The customer's monthly renewal payment should not exceed 30% of net monthly income. Every title pawn, at any amount, requires Joshua Davis's (CEO) approval before funding and a completed Large Loan/Buy Worksheet at $1,000 and above.
TITLE, PERFECTION AND DEFAULT — VIRGINIA REQUIREMENTS. A title pawn is not complete when the underwriting is done. Virginia law (Va. Code § 54.1-4005) requires that where a motor vehicle is pawned, the owner complies with Va. Code § 46.2-637, and that on default the Company complies with Va. Code § 46.2-633 and the lien provisions of Va. Code § 46.2-636 et seq.
In practice this means: the Company's security interest must be properly recorded against the title at origination, and the statutory default and sale process must be followed before any pawned vehicle is sold. A vehicle whose lien was never properly perfected cannot be lawfully sold on default, however sound the original underwriting was.
Because the perfection and default steps carry legal consequence, EVERY title pawn — at any dollar amount — requires Joshua Davis's (CEO) approval before funding, and any default on a title pawn is referred to him before any repossession or sale step is taken. No store may begin a default or sale process on a vehicle on its own.

#### 05.0 Charges, Rates and Disclosure

#### NO CHARGE MAY BE APPLIED TO A PAWN TRANSACTION THAT IS NOT ON THE LIST BELOW. Virginia law authorises four charges and caps each one. Bravo is configured to calculate them; staff do not compute or negotiate them by hand, and no store may add a fee of its own.

#### 1. INTEREST (Va. Code § 54.1-4008). Capped by loan size, per month: 10% per month on a loan of $25 or less; 7% per month on a loan of more than $25 and less than $100; 5% per month on a loan of $100 or more. A loan may never be divided into smaller loans to reach a higher percentage. Loans may be renewed based on the original loan amount, but may not be written so as to compound interest or storage fees from a previous loan on the same item.

#### 2. STORAGE FEE (Va. Code § 54.1-4013). A monthly storage fee may be charged for items requiring storage, not to exceed 5% of the amount loaned on that item.

#### 3. SERVICE FEE (Va. Code § 54.1-4009(C)(1)). Covers the daily law-enforcement reporting, the electronic record, and investigating title to the property. Capped at the LESSER of 5% or $3, computed on the amount loaned on the item or the amount the Company paid for it — so it applies to outright buys as well as loans.

#### 4. LATE FEE (Va. Code § 54.1-4009(C)(2)). Up to 10% of the amount loaned, for each item not claimed by the pledged date. This fee is lawful ONLY if the customer was notified of it on the pawn ticket. Confirm the fee appears on the printed ticket before it is charged.

#### LOST TICKET FEE (Va. Code § 54.1-4004). A lost or stolen pawn ticket carries a $5.00 fee, and this fee is lawful only if the customer was notified of it on the ticket. (This replaces the $2.00 figure in earlier editions of this manual.)

#### REDEEMING DURING THE GRACE PERIOD. A customer redeeming during the grace period pays the interest, storage and service charges actually accrued under the caps above, plus the late fee if one applies and was disclosed on the ticket. Bravo computes this figure. Earlier editions of this manual stated a flat 'minimum charge of 12.5% of the principal balance'; that figure is WITHDRAWN and must not be used, because it was not tied to any charge Virginia authorises. If Bravo's computed figure ever looks wrong, stop and call Preston Peters — do not substitute a percentage.

#### TRUTH IN LENDING — DISCLOSURE BEFORE SIGNING. A pawn loan is closed-end consumer credit and is subject to the federal Truth in Lending Act and Regulation Z. Before the customer signs, the pawn ticket must disclose the amount financed, the finance charge, the annual percentage rate (APR) and the total of payments. The finance charge and the APR include the storage and service fees — not only the interest line. Va. Code § 54.1-4008(B) confirms that an APR computed and disclosed under the federal Truth-in-Lending Act is not a violation of Virginia's rate caps.

#### In practice: Bravo prints these disclosures on the pawn ticket. Explain them to the customer in plain language before they sign — what they are borrowing, what it will cost them, and what they will owe to redeem. Never quote a customer a cost that differs from the printed ticket.

#### WHY THIS MATTERS. Any violation of Virginia's pawnbroker statutes is a Class 4 misdemeanor and is also a prohibited practice under the Virginia Consumer Protection Act (Va. Code § 54.1-4014), which allows a customer to sue directly. Understating an APR by treating fees as something other than a finance charge has been the subject of federal enforcement against pawnbrokers. If anything about a charge is unclear, ask before charging it.

#### ABOUT THE ITEM: a description of the item, its serial number, and a STATEMENT OF OWNERSHIP from the customer.

#### ABOUT THE TRANSACTION: the time, date and place; the amount loaned or the purchase price paid; the rate of interest on the loan; EACH FEE CHARGED, ITEMISED SEPARATELY (see Section 05.0); and the full terms and conditions of the loan, including the period of the loan.

#### ABOUT THE PERSON: full name, residence address, telephone number, and driver's license number or other identification; plus a particular physical description — height, weight, date of birth, race, gender, hair colour, eye colour, and any other identifying marks.

An item taken in without one cannot be lawfully sold when the loan defaults, no matter how much time has passed. Capture it at the time of the transaction, every time.

PAWNED FIREARMS — REDEMPTION IS A TRANSFER. Redeeming a pawned firearm is a transfer to a non-licensee and is handled exactly like a sale: the customer completes ATF Form 4473 AND the Virginia State Police consent form, and the store obtains a Virginia State Police background check BEFORE the firearm is handed back. There is NO permit that exempts anyone from this check in Virginia — a Virginia concealed handgun permit does NOT waive it. Virginia is a point-of-contact state: the check goes to the Virginia State Police Firearms Transaction Center (VCheck), NOT to ATF and NOT directly to FBI NICS. Record the VSP approval/reference number on the paperwork.
If the State Police respond 'delayed', the firearm is held. Under Va. Code § 18.2-308.2:2(B)(2), if the State Police tell the dealer a response will not be available by the end of the dealer's FIFTH BUSINESS DAY, the transfer may then be completed — count business days from the request and note December 25 does not count. If the response is 'denied', the firearm is NOT released to that person, in any circumstance, and the denial is noted on the loan. Tell the customer they may contest the denial with the State Police within 30 days; do not speculate about the reason. Any firearm redeemed must be supplied with a gun lock.
IF THE PLEDGOR IS DENIED. The loan continues on its normal terms — a denial does not cancel the loan. The pledgor may not receive the firearm. The pledgor may, in writing, designate another person to redeem the firearm on their behalf; that person must complete their own Form 4473 and Virginia State Police check and pay the loan in full, and the transfer is recorded as a transfer to that person. This is the ONE exception to the rule in Section 05.04 that a third party may never redeem a firearm, and it requires Preston Peters (Market Manager) approval on each occasion. If no lawful transferee redeems the firearm, the loan runs to default under Section 06.01 and the firearm becomes Company inventory.

### 05.05 Layaway Transactions
To process: select 'Transactions,' search by inventory number, select 'Layaway,' set the price, term and payment schedule on the Layaway Detail screen — entering the agreed final payment date correctly — review, choose payment type, and Save. Two receipts print and are signed; the original goes to the customer and the copy is filed in the daily transaction folder.
LAYAWAY IS FREE. Valley Pawn charges NO interest, NO service fee and NO holding fee on a layaway — this is a long-standing company commitment, it is advertised to customers, and every employee is expected to tell every customer about it. The customer puts a down payment on the item, pays it off over time, and takes it home when it is paid in full. Never quote a customer a layaway fee or interest charge of any kind.
DOWN PAYMENT AND TERM — THREE TIERS. The down payment and the length of the layaway depend on what the item is and what it costs. Jewelry has its own terms at any price; everything else is set by price. Quote these figures and no others.
JEWELRY — any price: 10% down, up to 10 MONTHS to pay.
EVERYTHING ELSE UNDER $500: 20% down, up to 3 MONTHS to pay.
EVERYTHING ELSE $500 AND OVER: 10% down, up to 6 MONTHS to pay.
The $500 line is read as: under $500.00 takes the 20% / 3-month terms; $500.00 and above takes the 10% / 6-month terms. An item priced at exactly $499.99 is UNDER $500 and takes 20% down over 3 months — price the tier off the actual tagged price, not a rounded one.
"Jewelry" means the jewelry categories in Section 04.03 — Pendants, Necklaces, Rings, Earrings, Bracelets. A watch, a coin or a bullion item is not jewelry for this purpose and takes the price-based tiers.
Set the term at initiation and enter the agreed final payment date in Bravo correctly — that date is what every later report measures against, and the weekly review flags items past it.
A PAYMENT IS DUE EVERY MONTH. The customer makes a payment each month for the life of the layaway. The term above is the OUTER limit, not a licence to skip months: a jewelry layaway is not "10 months of nothing then pay it off."
What that means in practice, and this is the part to explain to customers up front: miss ONE month and the layaway is past due — that customer goes on the call list and gets contacted. Miss TWO months in a row — 60 days with no payment — and the layaway EXPIRES under the rule below. The 60-day expiry can and does arrive before the outer term does; a customer who stops paying in month two of a 10-month jewelry layaway loses it in month four, not month ten.
EXPIRY — THE CONTROLLING RULE. A layaway with NO PAYMENT FOR 60 OR MORE DAYS is expired and must be processed as expired. This is the operative trigger the Company enforces and reports on weekly. It runs alongside the term limits above and is independent of them — whichever comes first governs. A customer who keeps paying monthly keeps the layaway alive for the full term.
On expiry the customer is issued STORE CREDIT for what they have paid. Valley Pawn does not simply keep the money. Store credit may be applied to another item and may be converted to a gift card. Where a customer changes their mind and asks to cancel, handle it the same way — cancel the layaway and issue the credit so the customer can put it toward something else.
Before an item is returned to sellable inventory, its layaway tag is removed in Bravo and the item is re-merchandised. Notify Preston Peters the same day if an expired layaway cannot be located.
THE FOUR NUMBERS REVIEWED EVERY WEEK. These are pulled from Bravo and published in the weekly layaway review, and each store is accountable for them:
Overdue — past the agreed final payment date. Work these first; they are the ones already out of time.
No payment in 30 days — the early-warning list. Call these customers before they become 60-day expiries. Getting layaways to pay is a store performance expectation, not an optional courtesy.
No payment in 60+ days — must be expired per policy (see above).
Locate — items the system cannot account for physically. This number must be ZERO. Any Locate layaway is found and resolved immediately and is escalated to Preston Peters the same day if it cannot be.

Two receipts print and are signed — the original to the customer, the copy filed in the daily transaction folder. Items on layaway are tagged and segregated from active retail inventory so they cannot be sold out from under the customer.
Firearms on layaway: the firearm is NOT transferred until it is paid in full, and the full Form 4473 and NICS process happens at pickup, not at layaway initiation. See Sections 04.02 and 05.02.

### eBay Listing-Age Standard (Reprice & Pull) — 90 / 120 / 150
Effective: July 4, 2026; ladder amended by Joshua Davis September 17, 2026 after store feedback. Applies to everyone who creates or manages eBay listings. Owner: Joshua Davis.
Why: eBay is a buy-it-now marketplace — fresh listings sell, stale ones get buried. Company sales data shows the median item sells in 6 days and 94% sell within 90 days. Cutting price before day 90 was giving away margin on items still selling at full price, so nothing is touched before then.
The ladder:
- Days 0–89: HANDS OFF. No reprice, no cut, no offers to watchers. Get the title, photos and item specifics right at listing time instead.
- Day 90, no sale: first cut of about 10% via Markdown Manager, after a sold-comp check.
- Day 120: second reduction of about 10%, or end and relist fresh if it has no watchers.
- Day 150: pull or final relist — one last relist with a materially new price, title and photos, or pull it from eBay to in-store retail, bundle or liquidate. Cuts stop at the 30% floor (three cuts maximum), at least 25 days apart; at the floor plus 14 days with no sale, the listing is ended.
In one line: nothing before 90, first cut at 90, second at 120, pull or final relist at 150.
FOUR RULES THAT BIND EVERY LISTING AT ANY AGE (Joshua Davis, September 17, 2026):
1. Best Offer is never switched on by anyone. A listing marked 'no offers' stays that way — that is the store's decision at listing time. Video games, consoles and game accessories NEVER take offers, at any price or age.
2. PRECIOUS METAL IS NEVER REPRICED ONLINE — gold, silver, platinum, bullion, coins, anything sold by weight. The counter prices metal at or just above melt, so there is no margin for a markdown to eat. Aged metal gets a person's decision, never a percentage cut. (This is the online rule. In-store aged-jewelry markdowns in Section 06.03 apply to retail-priced jewelry, and metal priced at melt is never marked down below melt anywhere.)
3. A manufacturer's model number is never removed from a title. Only the Company's own stock code — VAP, VP, VA, CUL, ROA, WAY, HAR or LEX followed by five or more digits, e.g. (VAP031234) — comes out of a title, and it goes into the listing's custom label, never deleted. A code like (A2482) or (DCD771) is the manufacturer's model number and one of the most-searched things in the title. When in doubt, leave it in.
4. Delisting is done through Bravo so the eBay link stays in sync (Section 5A above).
Support: a weekly report in #ebay-performance flags each store's listings approaching 90, 120 and 150 days, so there is a specific worklist rather than a pile to sort through.

## Section 8: Collections

### 08.01 Pawn Collection Calls
REDEMPTION REMINDERS. A pawn loan is non-recourse (Section 09.03): the customer owes nothing and is never told otherwise. Reminder calls exist to help customers keep the items they want to keep. Calls begin seven days before the due date and continue, no more than one call per day, until the due date or until contact is made. No message is left on voicemail. The same limits apply after the due date during the hold period in Section 06.01.

### 08.02 FDCPA
The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA), a federal law enacted in 1977 in response to widespread abusive, deceptive, and unfair debt collection practices, guides all Valley Pawn collection procedures. Following the FDCPA protects both debtors and the company.

#### Hours & Frequency of Contact
Debtors may only be contacted Monday through Saturday, and not before 8:00 AM or after 9:00 PM, unless the customer requests otherwise. Do not contact a debtor (or any reference) with a frequency or at hours that could reasonably be interpreted as threatening or harassing. Any reference who requests no further contact must be noted in the comment screen, and all contact with that reference must cease.

#### Privacy & Confidentiality
Do not discuss a debtor's account in front of anyone other than the debtor and their spouse. When mailing collection letters, do not indicate on the envelope that a debt is owed — use the plain office stamp (address only, no company name/phone number).

#### Disinterested Third Parties & Employers
Anyone other than the debtor, the debtor's spouse, or the debtor's attorney is a disinterested third party — never divulge account details or request payment from such a party. Do not communicate or threaten to communicate with a debtor's employer in a way that discloses the debt; honor any request (from the debtor or employer) for no workplace calls. Routine employment verification is not prohibited.

#### Prohibited Conduct
- Harassment — profane/obscene language, anonymous or collect calls, cash-on-delivery letters, repeated ringing believed to avoid the debtor, or any activity that abuses, ridicules, or degrades the consumer, or calls to the workplace against instruction.
- Impersonation — a collector must always identify with true company and personal name; no aliases, code names, or impersonation of any other person or agency.
- False, misleading, or deceptive tactics — regarding who you are, why you are calling, the status of the debt, or the action you intend to take.
- Threats — no statements threatening force, violence, criminal prosecution, or that legal action will cause the debtor to defend themselves in criminal court, or that the debtor's status will be disclosed to friends/relatives/employer/government.
- Trickery — offering references something of value for information, leaving misleading messages, vague claims of being a collection agency/credit bureau/attorney, or printed materials that could be mistaken for a summons, warrant, telegram, or check.

### 08.03 Past Due Loans - Pull Process
A past-due loan is one or more days late based on the due date. Reminder calls follow the Section 08.01 cadence; a contact is logged in Bravo only when direct contact is made, with any commitment noted.
If no contact is made, the item is held past the due date with reasonable contact attempts — no more than one per day — logged in Bravo. When the item reaches the pull point in Section 06.01, it moves to Pull From Inventory on that timeline, subject to the statement-of-ownership gate in that section. Section 06.01 is the only timeline; this section does not set a separate one.
Holding an item beyond the Section 06.01 timeline requires Joshua Davis's (CEO) approval, as stated there. Every effort must be made to inform the customer of the consequences of non-payment and the Company's right to sell the pledged item on default; after the statutory period has run and a statement of ownership is on file (Sections 05.00A and 09.03), the item becomes Company inventory.

## Section 9: Legal Information

### 09.01 General Policies
Pawn transactions involve a customer putting up an item as collateral for a loan. Under no circumstances can staff take legal action against a customer who has become delinquent on a pawn loan.

#### 09.02 Licensing and Bonding

#### Valley Pawn operates as a licensed pawnbroker in Virginia under Va. Code Title 54.1, Chapter 40. A pawnbroker licence is issued by the county, city or town, authorised by the local circuit court, and it names the specific BUILDING in which the business may be conducted — a licence does not travel to another address. The Company also maintains the surety bond Virginia requires of every licensed pawnbroker. Licences must be displayed prominently in the lobby and kept current (Section 03.05). Operating without a valid licence is a criminal offence, and each day is a separate offence.

#### As a federal firearms licensee at every store (each store holds its own Type 02 FFL), the Company is additionally subject to the Gun Control Act and ATF regulation. See Sections 04.02, 05.02 and 10.06.

#### 09.03 What Staff May and May Not Do on a Defaulted Loan

#### A pawn loan is NON-RECOURSE. The pledged item is the entire security for the loan. When a customer defaults, the Company's remedy is the item and nothing else.

#### Therefore: the Company does not sue a customer over a defaulted pawn, does not send an account to a collection agency, does not report it to a credit bureau, and does not pursue the customer for any shortfall if the item sells for less than the loan. Never tell a customer, or imply, that they owe a balance, that they may be sued, or that their credit will be affected. Those statements are untrue and making them can violate both federal collection standards (Section 08.02) and the Virginia Consumer Protection Act.

#### Upon proper default and expiry of the statutory holding and grace periods, and once a statement of ownership is on file, the item becomes the property of the Company and may be sold in the ordinary course of business (Va. Code § 54.1-4005). See Section 06.01 for the controlling timeline.

#### 09.04 Recordkeeping and Reporting Obligations

#### These are the legal obligations most likely to be breached by ordinary carelessness rather than bad intent, which is why they are listed together:

#### Transaction records — every field Va. Code § 54.1-4009 requires, captured at the time of the transaction. See Section 05.00A. Each omission is a Class 4 misdemeanor.

#### Daily law-enforcement report — filed by noon the following day, Va. Code § 54.1-4010. See Sections 03.15 and 05.00A.

#### Law-enforcement access — records and articles must be made available on request during business hours, Va. Code § 54.1-4011. See Section 01.06.

#### Federal firearms records — Form 4473, the Acquisition and Disposition record, and multiple-sale reporting on Form 3310.4. See Sections 04.02 and 05.02, and the retention rules in Section 03.08.

#### Cash reporting — IRS/FinCEN Form 8300 for cash received over $10,000, filed within 15 days, with a written statement to the customer by January 31 of the following year. See Section 05.02. Structuring a transaction to avoid the threshold is a federal crime and a terminable offence.

#### Truth in Lending — the cost of credit disclosed before the customer signs. See Section 05.0.

#### Data breach notification — Va. Code § 18.2-186.6. See Section 03.09.

#### 09.05 Charges and Consumer Protection

#### Virginia caps every charge a pawnbroker may make (Section 05.0). Beyond the caps themselves, two consequences are worth knowing. First, any violation of the pawnbroker chapter is a Class 4 misdemeanor and, on a second or subsequent offence, the court may suspend or revoke the licence. Second, Va. Code § 54.1-4014(B) makes any such violation a prohibited practice under the Virginia Consumer Protection Act, which lets a customer sue the Company directly. A fee that looks like a small internal matter is therefore both a criminal and a civil exposure.

#### Property in pawn may not be disfigured, and its identity may not be destroyed or affected, while it is in the Company's possession (Va. Code § 54.1-4012). This includes altering it in an effort to read a serial number. Normal cleaning and protective storage under Sections 04.03 and 05.01 are expected and are not affected by this rule.

#### Employment matters — at-will status, equal opportunity, harassment and discrimination, accommodation, leave, drug and alcohol policy, and the complaint procedure — are governed by the EMPLOYEE HANDBOOK, not by this manual. This manual covers operational and transactional procedure. Where the two appear to conflict on an employment question, the Handbook controls and the conflict should be reported to Preston Peters (Market Manager) so it can be fixed rather than worked around.

#### No employee is expected to resolve a legal question at the counter. Stop the transaction and call Preston Peters (Market Manager) — or Joshua Davis (CEO) if Preston is unreachable — whenever: a customer threatens legal action; law enforcement asks for anything beyond routine record access; an item may be stolen or its serial number looks altered; a customer appears to be structuring transactions to stay under a reporting threshold; a firearm transaction raises any doubt at all; a subpoena, court order, garnishment, bankruptcy notice or regulator letter arrives; or something simply feels wrong. ## Section 11: Glossary

### 11.01 Glossary of Terms
- Buy Transaction: A transaction in which the customer's item is bought, rather than used as collateral for a pawn loan.
- Finance Fee: The amount of funds charged to the customer for loaning money on their item; the cost of borrowing.
- Grace Period: the 15-day statutory period after a loan's term during which the item may not be sold (Va. Code § 54.1-4005). Company practice holds items longer — see Section 06.01.
- Gun Lock: An important tool used to prevent a firearm from being discharged while the device is in place.
- Karat: The measure of the purity of gold.
- Layaway: A customer pays a deposit to secure an item for later purchase.
- Merchandising: Prepping items to be sold via storefront or internet mediums.
- Pawn: A transaction in which a customer puts up an item as collateral to borrow money.
- Pawn Ticket: The customer's contract and proof of pawn, used when redeeming or refinancing an item.
- Profit: Total amount of fees earned minus any outstanding expenses.
- Qualifying: The process of determining an item's value and what a customer wants to do with it (pawn or sell).
- Redemption: A transaction in which a customer pays the loan amount plus finance fees to reclaim their pawned item.
- Refinance: Taking out a new pawn loan by paying the current finance charges on an existing loan, without redeeming the item outright.
- PFI (Pull for Inventory): The process of pulling defaulted pawn collateral from loan status into sellable store inventory (see Section 06.01).
- NCO / NCOM: Neat, Clean, and Orderly (later expanded internally to include Merchandised, and further to Professional) — the storefront-standards checklist referenced in Section 03.16.
- MHG: Multiple Handgun (Sales) — see the Multiple Handgun Sales Protocol in Sections 04.02 and 10.06.
- 4473 / 3310.4: ATF firearm transaction forms — see Sections 04.02 and 05.02.
- FFL: Federal Firearms License — every Valley Pawn store holds its own Type 02 (Pawnbroker) FFL, so every store takes, holds and transfers firearms under federal and Virginia law. The licence is specific to the building it names; the FFL registry, renewal calendar and vendor copies are maintained by the CEO.
- FDCPA: Fair Debt Collection Practices Act — see Section 08.02.

## Employment Classifications
At the time of hire, an employee is classified as a full-time, part-time or temporary employee. In addition, an employee is classified as either exempt or non-exempt. All other policies described in this handbook, the Policies & Procedures Manual, and other communications from the company apply to all employees, with the exception of certain wage, salary, and time-off limitations applying only to non-exempt employees. If an employee is unsure of their employment classification, he or she should contact their immediate supervisor. Sales and Loan Associates and Representatives are hourly (non-exempt). Store Managers may be hourly or salaried, as stated in their offer of employment. Any overtime must be approved by the employee’s supervisor in advance.

### Temporary Employees
The company may hire employees for specific periods of time, or for a specific project. These employees are considered temporary employees. The job assignment, work schedule, and duration of the position will be determined on an individual basis.
Normally a temporary position does not exceed six (6) months in duration, unless specifically extended by a written agreement. A temporary employee does not become a regular VP employee by virtue of being employed longer than the agreed-upon specified period.
Temporary employees are not eligible for benefits described in this handbook, except as required by local, state, and federal laws.
Temporary employees who are classified as non-exempt will receive overtime pay for any time worked over forty (40) hours during any workweek. All overtime must be approved by a supervisor.

### Full-Time Employees
An employee who has successfully completed his or her 90-day introductory period and who works at least thirty-two (32) hours per week is considered a full-time employee. Unless otherwise specified, the employee benefits described in this handbook apply only to full-time employees. The company does not provide holiday pay, paid sick leave, or paid jury-duty leave to any position.

### Part-Time Employees
An employee who works less than thirty-two (32) hours per week is considered a part-time employee. Part-time employees are not eligible for employee benefits described in this handbook, except to the extent required by local, state, and federal laws.

### Exempt Employees
An employee is classified as an exempt employee at his or her time of hire, transfer, and/or promotion. Exempt employees generally are, but are not limited to being, salaried employees who meet the duties and salary tests under the Fair Labor Standards Act. Exempt employees are not entitled to overtime pay.

### Non-Exempt Employees
An employee is classified as a non-exempt employee at his or her time of hire, transfer, and/or promotion. Non-exempt employees are, but are not limited to being, hourly paid employees. By law, non-exempt employees are entitled to overtime pay for hours worked in excess of forty (40) hours per workweek. The employee’s supervisor must approve all overtime in advance. Overtime worked without advance approval will be paid, and may result in corrective action.

### Introductory Period
An employee’s first ninety (90) days of employment with the company are considered an introductory period. During this period an employee is not eligible for any benefits described in this handbook unless otherwise required by law. The introductory period is a time for the employee to become familiar with the company, its services, job duties, policies, procedures, co-workers and supervisors.
The introductory period is a time for both the employee and the company to become familiar. The company will evaluate the employee’s suitability for continued employment while the employee evaluates the company. Completion of the introductory period does not guarantee continued employment. All employment is at-will. At the end of the introductory period the employee’s supervisor may review the employee’s job performance and discuss the results with the employee.

### Outside Employment
An employee must not take an outside job, either for pay or as a donation of their personal time, with a customer or a competitor of the company. An employee must not do work on their own if it competes in any way with the sales of products or services that the company provides or performs. Before an employee accepts any outside employment, the employee is strongly encouraged to discuss the matter with his or her immediate supervisor. This policy is a conflict-of-interest guideline, not a non-compete agreement, and does not restrict any activity protected under Virginia law, including Va. Code § 40.1-28.7:8 (which prohibits non-compete agreements for low-wage and overtime-eligible employees). Nothing in this policy limits an employee’s right to seek or accept other employment after their employment with the Company ends.

### Former Employees and Rehire
Depending on the circumstances, the company may consider a former employee for re-employment. Such applicants are subject to the company’s usual pre-employment procedures. To be considered, an applicant must have been in good standing at the time of their separation from the company.
If an employee is rehired less than thirty (30) days from their separation date, the employee’s original hire date will be restored and any elected employee benefits such as medical or dental will be restored. PTO is not reinstated; PTO is not paid out at separation and does not carry across a break in service. If the employee is rehired more than thirty (30) days after separation, the hire date will be the date of rehire.

### Relatives / Employee Relationships
Although the company may hire an employee’s relative, the situation is closely reviewed in advance and closely monitored during employment. An employee must not directly supervise their relative. The goal of the company is to provide a professional service and/or product to its customers. It is also the goal of the company to provide its employees with a comfortable work environment; therefore, it is imperative that a situation that may cause conflict be avoided.
If the company determines, at its sole discretion, that a relationship between any employees causes (or has the potential to cause) a conflict of interest, the appearance of such a conflict, or an adverse effect on an employee’s efficiency or performance, the company may reassign or, where no reasonable alternative exists, separate any or all employees involved.

## Work Schedules and Attendance
All employees are expected to report to work as scheduled and on time. Employees are expected to work until the workday ends. Any deviations from scheduled hours must be approved through the employee’s immediate supervisor.

### Hours of Business
Store hours are 10:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. Culpeper is open Monday through Saturday. Harrisonburg, Waynesboro, Lexington, and Roanoke are open Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, and Saturday, and are closed Wednesday. All stores are closed Sunday. Store personnel must be in the store preparing to open no later than 9:30 a.m. and remain until all customers have been served and closing duties are complete (see Policies & Procedures Manual 01.01).
The company is a service and retail company and must have liberal hours of business in order to meet the needs of our customers. Work hours will, therefore, vary from location to location. The supervisor will ensure that the necessary work hours are set and remain within the limits of local, state and federal laws.

### Time Tracking — Clocking In and Out
Each employee is responsible for accurately recording his or her time worked. No one may record another employee’s time, or clock in or clock out for any other employee. Tampering with or altering another employee’s time record is cause for disciplinary action, up to and including termination of any or all employees involved. In the event of an actual error, the employee must report the matter to their immediate supervisor immediately. All time actually worked will be paid, including time worked without clocking in or without advance approval; working off the clock is prohibited and will result in corrective action, but the time will still be paid.

### Lunch and Break Periods
Each employee will be given two (2) ten-minute paid breaks each workday. The employee does not clock out for these breaks. Ideally these breaks should be taken at two different intervals, one in the morning and one in the afternoon. All break times, if not directly assigned by a supervisor, should be coordinated with co-workers to maintain adequate coverage.
Meal periods and breaks follow Policies & Procedures Manual 01.01. In summary: every employee takes a thirty (30) minute lunch each day. Where more than one employee is on duty, lunches are staggered, the employee is fully relieved of duty, clocks out, and the lunch is unpaid. Where only one employee is on duty, that employee stays responsible for the store and is not relieved of duty, so the thirty minutes is paid time worked — do not clock out. All breaks are completed by 2:00 p.m. (or 3:00 p.m. if arranged in advance with the Market Manager), using the break button in the timekeeping app. If an unpaid lunch is interrupted by work, clock back in; interrupted time is paid.

### Attendance and Punctuality
Valley Pawn stores operate with small crews, and many shifts are single-coverage at the counter. An unfilled shift means a closed counter. Reliable attendance is an essential function of every store position, and the standards below are strict for that reason.
Calling in. If you cannot report for a scheduled shift, or will be late, you must call Preston Peters, Market Manager, by telephone at the number provided to you at hire, at least two (2) hours before the shift starts. A phone call is the only accepted method. Text messages, emails, Slack messages, and messages passed through co-workers do not count. If the call is not answered, leave a voicemail and keep calling until you reach him; a voicemail alone is not a completed call-in unless he confirms it. Contact is required each day of an absence unless a longer absence has been approved in advance.
Tardiness. Arrival more than seven (7) minutes after the scheduled start without prior approval is a tardy.
No-call / no-show. Failure to report for a scheduled shift without calling in as described above is a no-call/no-show. Any no-call/no-show will result in immediate termination. The only exception is an employee who was physically unable to call (for example, hospitalized or incapacitated) and who provides documentation of that as soon as reasonably possible.
Excessive absenteeism. One (1) or more unscheduled absences, or two (2) or more tardies, in any rolling ninety (90) day period is excessive and will result in corrective action up to and including termination. This standard applies from the first day of employment, including the Introductory Period. It is applied the same way to every employee.
Illness. Do not work while sick. An absence for illness follows the call-in procedure above and counts as an unscheduled absence unless it is a protected absence. A note from a healthcare provider is required for every absence due to illness or a medical reason, starting with the first day, and must be given to the Market Manager on the day you return. The note must confirm that you were seen and the dates you were unable to work; it does not need to state a diagnosis. The company does not provide paid sick leave; see Sick Leave and Unpaid Personal and Medical Leave in the Benefits section.
Protected absences. Absences covered by an approved accommodation under the ADA or the Virginia Human Rights Act, by workers’ compensation, by jury or witness duty, by military service, by service as a volunteer emergency responder during a declared emergency (Va. Code § 40.1-27.5), or by any other legally protected leave do not count toward these thresholds and are not grounds for corrective action. Employees who believe an absence is protected must tell the Market Manager or Human Resources so it can be recorded correctly.
Jury and witness duty. Employees summoned for jury duty or subpoenaed as a witness will be given the time off required. Virginia law (Va. Code § 18.2-465.1) prohibits the company from discharging or taking adverse action against an employee for jury service and from requiring the employee to use PTO for it; the company complies with that law. Jury duty leave is unpaid. An employee who wishes to use available PTO for jury duty may do so voluntarily. Give the Market Manager a copy of the summons as soon as you receive it.

It is the company’s desire to pay all regular employees wages and salaries that are competitive with other employers in the marketplace and that will be motivational, fair, and equitable. The company applies the same standards and principles to all employees, regardless of organizational level, race, color, religion, national origin, sex, pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions (including lactation), age, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, veteran or military status, or any other factor protected by law. These protections are consistent with the Virginia Human Rights Act, as amended by the Virginia Values Act.

### Paydays
The payroll week begins on Sunday and ends on Saturday. The company computes time on the basis of a forty-hour workweek. Paydays are weekly on Friday for services performed during the week ending the previous Saturday. The weekly pay schedule consists of fifty-two (52) pay periods per year. Changes will be made and announced in advance whenever company holidays or closings interfere with the normal pay schedule.

### Administrative Updates and Pay Corrections
The company takes all reasonable steps to ensure that employees receive the correct amount of pay in each paycheck and that employees are paid promptly on the scheduled payday. In the unlikely event that there is an error in the amount of pay, the employee should promptly bring the discrepancy to the attention of the immediate supervisor or Human Resources so corrections can be made as quickly as possible.

### Personnel Data Changes
It is the responsibility of each employee to promptly notify the company of any changes in personnel data. Personal mailing addresses, telephone numbers, number and names of dependents, individuals to be contacted in the event of an emergency, and other such information should be accurate and current at all times. Update your information in Gusto or notify Human Resources in writing.

### Direct Deposit and Final Pay
The company offers direct deposit of an employee’s pay into a designated financial institution account of the employee’s choice. Employees who choose direct deposit will receive a pay statement in Gusto that details compensation, authorized deductions such as taxes and benefit costs, the amount deposited, and year-to-date earnings and deductions. Direct deposit is highly recommended.
When employment ends for any reason, all wages earned through the last day worked will be paid on or before the next regular payday, by the employee’s usual payment method (Va. Code § 40.1-29). Physical final checks will be mailed to the address on file.

### Mandatory Payroll Deductions
The company is required by law to make certain deductions from employees’ pay each period, including federal, state, and local taxes and Social Security and Medicare deductions. All deductions are itemized on the pay statement. The amount of income tax withheld depends on the employee’s earnings and on the information provided on their Form W-4. An employee who wishes to change their federal withholding must complete a new Form W-4 in Gusto. An employee who claims exempt status from withholding must file a new Form W-4 by February 15 each year to continue the exemption.

### Wage Garnishments
When court-ordered deductions are required to be withheld from an employee’s paycheck, the employee will be notified and the deduction explained. The company acts in accordance with the federal Consumer Credit Protection Act and state law, which place restrictions on the total amounts that may be garnished from an employee’s paycheck.

### Social Security
Wage earners are required by law to contribute a set percentage of their wages to Social Security and Medicare, and the company is required to deduct this amount from the employee’s paycheck each pay period and to match the employee’s contribution. Since the employee’s Social Security number is used to record their earnings, the employee must verify that the Social Security number on their pay statement and Form W-2 is correct. Employees may review their earnings record at www.ssa.gov or by calling 1-800-772-1213.

### Overtime Pay
All non-exempt employees are eligible to receive pay of one-and-one-half times their regular rate for hours worked over forty (40) in one workweek. The employee’s supervisor must approve all overtime in advance; unapproved overtime will be paid but may result in corrective action. If an employee is absent due to a job-related injury, a holiday closure, jury duty, or PTO, the hours not worked will not be counted as hours worked for the purpose of computing overtime.

### Bonus Pay
Bonus pay is determined by the company for each category and type of employee, and is governed by the Monthly Bonus Program as published in the Policies & Procedures Manual and announced in Slack. The company may modify the program at any time.

### Performance Reviews
The company wants each employee to grow and to succeed in their position with the company; therefore, formal performance reviews are normally conducted once per year on each employee. An employee’s performance may also be reviewed near the end of their introductory period, or in the event of a promotion or a change in duties and/or responsibilities.
The performance review is an opportunity for collaborative communication between the employee and the supervisor. This is a good time for an employee to discuss any interests and/or future goals and for a supervisor to recommend further training or additional opportunities. Questions regarding the review process should be directed to the employee’s supervisor.

### Compensation Reviews
Compensation reviews are usually given at the same time as a performance review. A compensation review does not always mean that there will be an increase in pay. Any pay increases will depend on how consistently and effectively an employee performs over a given period of time. In addition to individual job performance reviews, the company periodically reviews job descriptions to ensure that changes in the duties and responsibilities of each position are recognized and adequately compensated.

### Promotion, Transfer, and Relocation
When practical, the company attempts to provide existing employees the opportunity to advance to other positions by promoting from within. When it is necessary to recruit applicants from outside, the company utilizes all available sources of qualified personnel. All decisions regarding recruitment, selection, and placement of employees are made solely on the basis of job-related criteria, training, experience, work record, and similar factors. Promotions and/or transfers are made without regard to race, color, religion, national origin, sex, pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions (including lactation), age, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, veteran or military status, or any other factor protected by law. All requests for consideration for a position must be directed to the employee’s immediate supervisor.
The company retains the right to exercise customary managerial functions, including the right to transfer employees between stores and classifications. The company, at its sole discretion, may elect to assist a newly hired or existing employee with relocation expenses.

## Benefits
In addition to receiving an equitable wage and having an equal opportunity for professional development and advancement, the employee may be eligible for other benefits. The company will periodically review the benefits program and will make modifications as necessary. The company reserves the right to modify, add to, or delete any benefit it currently offers.

### Benefits Eligibility
All full-time employees are offered participation in the benefits described in this handbook as soon as they have met the eligibility requirements for each particular benefit.
A part-time employee will only be offered those benefits that are specifically required by law. Temporary employees are not eligible for benefits.

### Sick Leave
The Company does not currently provide paid sick leave. Time off for illness or injury may be taken as PTO (if the employee has accrued PTO and the absence otherwise complies with the PTO policy) or as unpaid leave under the Unpaid Personal and Medical Leave policy below. Employees are never required to work while sick, and an absence for illness that follows the call-in procedure in the Attendance and Punctuality policy is not misconduct. Employees needing extended medical leave should contact Human Resources to discuss unpaid leave and any legally protected leave that may apply (see Unpaid Personal and Medical Leave and Virginia Disability Accommodation Rights).
Note: Virginia’s paid sick leave law (2026 Acts of Assembly, HB 5 / SB 199) will require the Company to provide paid sick leave beginning January 1, 2029 (or January 1, 2028 if the Company employs 25 or more employees). This section will be updated before that date.

### Unpaid Personal and Medical Leave
Employees who need time off for their own illness or injury, for the illness of an immediate family member, or for a personal emergency, and who do not have available PTO, may request unpaid leave from Human Resources. Requests should be made as soon as the need is known and, where possible, before the absence. Approved unpaid leave does not count as an unscheduled absence under the Attendance and Punctuality policy.
Unpaid leave may also be provided as a reasonable accommodation under the ADA and the Virginia Human Rights Act; see Virginia Disability Accommodation Rights.
A note from a healthcare provider is required for every absence due to illness or a medical reason, starting with the first day (see Attendance and Punctuality). For an absence of three (3) or more consecutive scheduled workdays, the note must also confirm that the employee is able to return to work, with or without restrictions. The note does not need to state a diagnosis.
Unpaid leave is approved for a defined period. An employee who does not return at the end of an approved leave, and who has not requested and received an extension, will be considered to have voluntarily resigned. Whether the employee’s position will be held open depends on the length of the leave and the needs of the store; the Company will tell the employee in writing when the leave is approved.

### Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA)
As of this handbook’s effective date, the Company employs fewer than 50 employees within a 75-mile radius of any worksite and is therefore not currently a covered employer under the federal Family and Medical Leave Act. This section is included for transparency and will govern automatically, without further amendment, if and when the Company’s headcount within a 75-mile radius of a given worksite reaches the 50-employee threshold. Until then, employees needing extended medical leave should contact Human Resources to discuss available options, including PTO, Unpaid Personal and Medical Leave, and any other legally required leave (e.g., USERRA, Virginia disability/pregnancy accommodation). Eligible employees who have worked for the Company at least 12 months and at least 1,250 hours in the preceding 12 months may take up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave in a 12-month period for a serious health condition, the birth or placement of a child, or to care for a spouse, child, or parent with a serious health condition, or for a qualifying military-family reason. Contact Human Resources to request FMLA leave and required certification. The Company will restore eligible employees to the same or an equivalent position upon return, subject to FMLA’s limitations.
Note: Virginia’s Paid Family and Medical Leave program (2026 Acts of Assembly, HB 1207) will begin collecting payroll contributions April 1, 2028, with benefits available December 1, 2028. This handbook will be updated with the program details before contributions begin.

### Military Leave (USERRA)
The Company complies with the Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act (USERRA). Employees who leave work for uniformed service will not be discriminated against because of that service and are entitled to reemployment rights consistent with USERRA upon return, provided applicable notice and timing requirements are met.

### Health Insurance
The company does not currently offer group health, dental, or vision insurance to store positions. Employees may obtain individual coverage through the federal Health Insurance Marketplace (healthcare.gov); the company will provide the required Marketplace notice at hire. If the company offers a group health plan to any position in the future, eligibility, cost, and continuation-of-coverage rights will be communicated in writing at that time.

### Holidays
Stores are closed on three days each year: New Year’s Day, Thanksgiving Day, and Christmas Day. These are unpaid days off; the company does not provide holiday pay. Stores are open on all other holidays, and employees scheduled to work on those days work their normal shift at their normal rate.

### Paid Time Off (PTO)
Eligible full-time employees accrue PTO based on length of service: 40 hours annually after one (1) year of service; 80 hours annually after two (2) or more years of service. Once eligible, employees must use at least 40 consecutive hours (one full workweek) of PTO each calendar year. PTO requests must be submitted at least 30 days in advance through Gusto and are subject to business-needs approval. PTO may not be taken during blackout periods (November 28 – January 5, and February 1 – March 31), except for legally protected absences. Unused PTO does not roll over and is forfeited at year-end.
PTO is not paid out upon separation from employment for any reason, whether the employee resigns, is laid off, or is terminated. This has been the Company’s consistent practice and is permitted under Virginia law.

### Retirement Plan
The company sponsors the Full Circle Finance 401(k) Plan, a safe harbor 401(k) plan administered through Gusto (formerly Guideline). This section is a short summary only. The official plan document and Summary Plan Description (SPD) control in every case, and the company may amend or end the plan as the plan document allows.
Eligibility. Employees are eligible once they are at least eighteen (18) years old and have completed twelve (12) months of employment. Service is measured by elapsed time, so there is no minimum-hours requirement and part-time employees qualify on the same terms as full-time employees. Employees covered by a collective bargaining agreement, nonresident aliens with no U.S.-source income, leased employees, and residents of Puerto Rico are excluded as the plan document provides.
Entry. An eligible employee may begin contributing with the first payroll that is administratively feasible after meeting the requirements, and no later than two (2) pay periods or two (2) weeks after that date, whichever is later.
Automatic enrollment. Newly eligible employees are automatically enrolled at the default contribution rate stated in their enrollment notice (currently 3% of pay) unless they choose a different rate or opt out by the deadline in that notice. Contributions may be made pre-tax or as Roth contributions, and rollovers from other qualified plans are accepted.
Company contributions. The company makes a safe harbor matching contribution of 100% of the first 3% of pay an employee contributes, plus 50% of the next 2% of pay (a maximum match of 4% of pay for employees who contribute 5% or more). The company may also make a discretionary contribution as the plan document allows. All employee and company contributions are 100% vested at all times.
Notices and questions. Before an employee's eligibility date, and each year after that, Gusto sends the plan invitation, the SPD, and the required safe harbor and automatic-enrollment notices. Questions about the plan go to Human Resources.

### Workers’ Compensation
All employees are covered by workers’ compensation insurance. This company-paid coverage is automatic and immediate and protects the employee in case of on-the-job injury or illness. If an employee cannot work due to a job-related injury or illness, workers’ compensation insurance pays eligible medical bills and may provide a portion of the employee’s income until he or she can return to work.
All job-related injuries or illnesses, no matter how minor, must be reported immediately to the Store Manager, who will notify Human Resources the same day. Prompt reporting is essential and the key to prompt benefits. The company will pay wages for the remainder of the normal workday in which a work-related accident occurs. An employee returning to work after being absent due to a work-related injury must provide a doctor’s release prior to beginning the workday.

## Safety
The company is committed to the safety and health of all employees and to complying with the regulations governing injury and accident prevention. Maintaining a safe working environment requires the continuous efforts and cooperation of all employees. If an employee has any doubts about how to safely perform a job, or has any questions about a safety issue, it is the employee’s responsibility to raise them with their immediate supervisor or Human Resources. Any suspected unsafe condition and all injuries that occur on the job must be reported immediately to your supervisor. Compliance with safety rules is a condition of employment.

### Personal Safety
The following guidelines apply when making bank deposits or doing any work outside the store:
While traveling in an automobile, obey all traffic laws and wear a safety belt at all times.
Keep your cell phone with you at all times.
Before getting out of your vehicle, survey the area for people loitering and other hazards.
If a customer or anyone else becomes angry, threatening, or violent, leave the area and report the incident to your supervisor immediately.
Bank deposits should be made as soon as the store closes for the day. Conceal the bank bag in a plain bag or folder; never carry it where it is visible.
Do not accumulate large sums of cash before making a deposit. If necessary, make more than one deposit during the day.
Keep all cash drawers skimmed according to company policy and procedure throughout the workday.

### Lifting and Material Handling
When lifting an item, think “safety first”: bend your knees, hold the item close to your body, and space your feet for good balance. Ask for assistance when lifting heavy objects or moving heavy items.
Flammable items such as cleaning fluids and paint must be used with caution, stored in approved and labeled containers, and kept away from any ignition source. Materials must be stacked only to safe heights and never in aisles or near exits. Heavy or breakable items should not be stored on high shelves. Never throw or toss an item; place it carefully.

### Injury
Employees are responsible for reporting any injuries incurred on the job to their immediate supervisor immediately. Seek medical care for any serious injury immediately. In the event of an injury, management may request that you see a doctor or be taken to the emergency room; it is your responsibility to comply with the request.

### Property, Equipment, and Tools
It is the employee’s responsibility to understand how to operate and care for all equipment in the workplace. If any equipment or facility item is not in proper working order or appears unsafe, inform your supervisor immediately and do not operate it. Never adjust or modify the safeguards on any equipment. Use tools only for their intended purpose. Safety glasses must be worn when operating a power tool. Never stand on a wet floor while using an electrical device, never overload outlets, keep extension cords in good repair and out of walkways, and never make unauthorized electrical connections or repairs. When using a ladder, be sure of its stability and place it on a clean, dry surface.

### Housekeeping
Each employee is required to keep his or her work area neat and orderly at all times. Spills must be cleaned up and debris disposed of immediately. Aisles and stairways must be kept clean, clear, and well lit. Cabinet doors, file cabinets, and desk drawers must be closed when not in use. If anything — facility, equipment, plumbing, lighting — needs repair or replacement, inform your supervisor immediately.

### Workplace Inspections and Searches
The safety of all employees and customers is of utmost importance. To ensure a safe workplace, the company forbids the possession and use of weapons (other than company firearms inventory handled under company procedure) and illegal drugs on company premises. For this purpose, and for the purpose of audit and loss-prevention investigations, the company reserves the right to inspect any package, bag, or other article carried onto or off company property, and to search any company-owned desk, file, locker, computer, vehicle, or other area on company premises. All desks, files, and lockers are company property issued for use during employment. Employees should have no expectation of privacy in company property. Personal property in the workplace should be limited; the company is not responsible for personal items. Inspections may be conducted at any time at the discretion of the company.
When the company has reasonable suspicion that an employee is in possession of illegal drugs, alcohol, weapons, or stolen property, or has violated a company policy that requires it, a reasonable search may be requested of the employee’s personal belongings, including bags and personal vehicles brought onto company property. Searches will be conducted by a manager, with a witness where possible, and in a manner that respects the employee’s dignity. Employees will not be physically searched. When appropriate, an investigation may be turned over to law enforcement. An employee who refuses to cooperate with a search under this policy is subject to disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

### Violence in the Workplace
Any acts or threats of physical violence, including intimidation, harassment, and/or coercion, which involve or affect the company or which occur on company property will not be tolerated. Workplace violence includes, but is not limited to: hitting, pushing, shoving, or any other violent form of body contact; threatening an individual or his or her family, friends, associates, or property with harm; intentional destruction of, or the threat of destroying, personal or company property; making harassing or threatening phone calls, or sending harassing or threatening notes, voicemails, texts, or emails; harassing surveillance or stalking of an employee; and unauthorized possession or inappropriate use of firearms or weapons. This applies to conduct on company premises and to conduct off premises by a representative of the company.
Violations of this policy will lead to appropriate disciplinary action, up to and including termination, and may be referred to law enforcement. Every employee is encouraged to report any incident or threat of violence to their supervisor or Human Resources immediately. If there is an immediate danger, call 911 first.

### Fire and Emergency
In case of fire, explosion, serious accident, or other emergency requiring outside aid, call 911 or ask another employee to do so, then notify your supervisor.

### Substance Abuse Policy
To establish and maintain a safe, healthy working environment free of alcohol- and drug-related performance problems, accidents and injuries, VP prohibits the sale, offer to sell, purchase, use, transfer, or possession of illegal drugs while on Company business or on Company premises. The Company strictly prohibits employees from being under the influence of alcohol or impaired by any drug while performing Company business, including driving Company-owned or -leased vehicles. Violation of this rule will result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination.
Lawfully prescribed and over-the-counter medications are permitted to the extent that their use does not adversely affect the employee’s ability to work safely. If a medication may affect your ability to perform your job safely, tell Human Resources before your shift; you do not need to identify the medication or the medical condition. Human Resources will determine, in consultation with the employee, whether any temporary work adjustment is needed.
No alcoholic beverages may be brought onto or consumed on Company premises. The use of alcoholic beverages on Company premises or while conducting Company business is prohibited and may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination, even for a first offense.
Testing. All applicants are drug tested before starting work (see Pre-Employment Screening). Reasonable suspicion testing. The Company may require an employee to submit to a drug and/or alcohol test, in accordance with applicable law, when there is reasonable suspicion — based on specific, observable facts such as appearance, behavior, speech, or odor — that the employee is under the influence of drugs or alcohol at work, or following a workplace accident that causes injury or significant property damage. A refusal is treated as a positive result. For store-level employees a positive result or refusal ends employment the same day; for other employees it is grounds for termination. Test results are confidential and shared only with those who need to know.

### Cannabis
Employees may not possess or use cannabis in any form on company premises or during work hours, and may not be impaired by cannabis while performing company business.
Every store-level position handles firearms under the store’s federal firearms license. A positive drug test for THC, or a refusal to test, ends employment the same day for any store-level employee, whether the use occurred on or off duty and regardless of any Virginia medical cannabis certification (see Drug-Free Workplace and Cannabis Testing under Pre-Employment Screening). For Corporate Support positions that do not handle firearms, a positive THC test is grounds for termination, except that the company will not discipline an employee solely for lawful off-duty use of cannabis oil under a valid Virginia written certification (Va. Code § 40.1-27.4), provided the employee is not impaired at work and does not possess cannabis on company property.

### Workplace Monitoring
All company email, telephone calls, internet activity, and computer use are monitored and recorded. This includes calls made or received on store phone lines (including the Zoom Phone system), email on company accounts, web browsing on company devices or networks, messages on company systems, and activity on company computers and point-of-sale systems. Recordings and logs may be reviewed at any time for quality control, training, security, loss prevention, and investigation of policy violations, and may be provided to law enforcement. Employees have no expectation of privacy in any communication made on, or activity conducted through, a company system or device. Your use of any company telephone, computer, email, messaging, or network system is your consent to this monitoring and recording, and by signing the Acknowledgment at the end of this handbook you confirm that consent.
Virginia permits recording of a call with the consent of one party (Va. Code § 19.2-62). Some other states require all parties to consent; when you are on a recorded store line with a caller in another state, follow the store’s call-greeting procedure so the caller is informed.
The company conducts video surveillance of all non-private workplace areas, including sales floors, counters, safes, back rooms, and entrances. Video is used to identify safety concerns, detect theft and misconduct, and discourage harassment and workplace violence. Video is never recorded in restrooms.

## Corrective Action and Separation

### Corrective Action
All employment with the company is at-will. Any employee whose conduct is unacceptable or whose job performance is unsatisfactory may be dismissed without prior corrective action. The company may, at its discretion, use the following steps to give an employee the opportunity to correct a problem. These steps are not required, need not be followed in order, and do not create any contractual right.
Verbal warning. The employee’s supervisor discusses the problem or violation and communicates the expected remedy. The supervisor documents the warning and a copy is placed in the employee’s personnel file.
Written warning (Record of Conversation). A written warning may be issued for a violation of company policies, procedures, rules, or standards of conduct, or for repeated or more serious violations. The supervisor discusses the seriousness of the situation and the expected performance or behavior, documents the warning, and gives the employee an opportunity to respond in writing. A copy is placed in the employee’s personnel file. The Policies & Procedures Manual refers to this step as a “Record of Conversation.”
Suspension. If an employee commits any of the following, or any other action the company determines to be similarly serious, he or she may be terminated or, at the company’s discretion, suspended without pay pending investigation: theft; falsification of company records; failure to follow safety practices and procedures; breach of confidentiality; threats or acts of bodily harm; willful or negligent destruction of property; use or possession of intoxicants or illegal drugs at work; deliberate insubordination toward a supervisor; harassment of any kind. Following any investigation, the employee may be terminated without prior or additional corrective action.

### Termination
The company reserves the right to terminate employment at any time, with or without reason. An employee may be dismissed for failure to perform assigned tasks satisfactorily, for conduct detrimental to the company or its operations, for violation of any policy or procedure in this handbook or the Policies & Procedures Manual, or for no reason at all. Where an employee’s conduct may constitute a crime, the company may refer the matter to law enforcement and the Commonwealth’s Attorney.
As stated previously, the company operates under the principle of at-will employment. Neither the employee nor the company has entered into a contract regarding the duration of employment. The employee is free to end employment at any time, with or without reason, and the company would hope and expect two weeks’ notice in the event of a resignation. Final wages are paid as described under Direct Deposit and Final Pay. PTO is not paid out at separation for any reason. All company property, including shirts and keys, must be returned within 48 hours of your last day; see Return of Company Property.

### Post-Employment Inquiries
The company does not respond to verbal requests for employee references. Human Resources may provide a reference to potential employers only if the former employee has completed and signed a release form. An employee may not respond to any request for information regarding another employee, a former employee, or a job applicant unless it is part of his or her assigned job responsibilities; such requests must be forwarded to Human Resources.

### Return of Company Property
Employees are issued company property at hire, including company-branded shirts and store keys, and may be issued other items (phones, laptops, credit cards) during employment. All company property must be returned within forty-eight (48) hours after your last day of work, or immediately upon the company’s request, whichever comes first. Return items to the Store Manager or Market Manager and get a written receipt.
The company may deduct the replacement cost of any item not returned within 48 hours — including the cost of re-keying the store if keys are not returned — from your final paycheck, but only under the Company Property and Wage Deduction Authorization you sign at hire, which lists each item and its replacement cost. Virginia law (Va. Code § 40.1-29) requires that written, signed authorization for any deduction, and no deduction will reduce your pay below the federal minimum wage for the hours worked in that pay period. If you have not signed the authorization, or the amount exceeds what can lawfully be deducted, the company may recover the balance by other lawful means.
