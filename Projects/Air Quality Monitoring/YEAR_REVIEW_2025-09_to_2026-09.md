8,376 hourly samples over 353 days, pulled 2026-09-05 via the dashboard's own API from the logged-in

## Headline
The house has **no radon or CO2 problem**. The two things worth acting on are **humidity** (too high a third of
the year, especially June–October) and **particulates from daytime activity** (cooking-pattern PM2.5 spikes,
with one extreme episode June 8–10 2026). VOC sits in Airthings' "fair" band almost half the time but rarely
reaches a concerning level.

## Humidity — the biggest recurring issue
- 88 of 353 days averaged ≥60% RH (the level where dust-mite and mold growth accelerate). 36 days peaked ≥70%.
- Monthly averages: Sep 25 **58.6** · Oct **58.5** · Nov 54.5 · Dec 53.0 · Jan 47.5 · Feb 42.7 · Mar 53.6 · Apr 54.1 · May 56.5 · Jun **58.0** · Jul **60.3** · Aug **62.8** · Sep 26 56.0.
- Intraday: lowest 53.4% at midnight, rises to a 57% peak around 10 AM (morning showers + outdoor air), then holds ~55% all afternoon — i.e. the AC is not pulling moisture down during the day.
- The new Upstairs Vanity Mini read 64% on its first day (bathroom).
- What to do: keep HVAC fan on **Auto** (not On — a constantly running fan re-evaporates coil moisture); confirm the bathroom exhaust runs 20 min after showers (timer switch); target 45–55%. If Jun–Oct averages stay ≥58% next season, a whole-home dehumidifier on the air handler is the durable fix. Impact windows + tight envelope make this more likely, not less.

## Particulates (PM2.5 / PM1) — cooking-hour pattern plus one big episode
- Clear daily shape: ~4 µg/m³ overnight → **15 µg/m³ at 11 AM–noon**, second bump ~3 PM, back down by 9 PM. Highest on **Wed/Thu (9.7/9.5)**, lowest Sunday (7.0).
- 119 days had an hour ≥25 µg/m³; 39 days ≥50; 58 days averaged ≥12 (the EPA annual guideline is 9).
- **June 2026 was the worst month by far**: avg 15.7 (2× normal) with hourly peaks of 469 (Jun 8), 356 (Jun 9), 353 (Jun 10) and 295 (Jun 25). Three consecutive days that high looks like an indoor source (heavy cooking/searing, candles, a project) or smoke infiltration — worth remembering what happened that week.
- Other spikes: Oct 22 2025 (279), Sep 6 2025 (158 PM1), Dec 10 2025 (128 PM1).
- What to do: run the range hood on high *before* and 10 min after cooking; if there's no ducted hood, that is the single most effective upgrade. A HEPA purifier in the main living space knocks the 11 AM–3 PM plateau down.

## VOC
- Average 277 ppb, in Airthings' "fair" band (250–2000) 44% of hours, "poor" only 0.1%.
- Strong daily cycle: 214 ppb at 5 AM → 357 at 2 PM — tracks temperature and daytime activity (cleaning products, cooking, off-gassing from furnishings warms up with the house).
- Two outlier days: Oct 29 2025 (3,083) and Dec 17 2025 (2,298) — one-off events (paint/cleaning/new item). Worst months Jun 2026 (341) and Nov 2025 (322); best Sep (193).
- Kitchen Cab Mini's first readings are ~700 ppb — expected inside a cabinet with cleaning chemicals; that sensor is measuring the cabinet, not the room.

## Temperature
- Very steady: 69.7 °F at 6 AM → 74.3 °F at 2 PM. Coolest month Jan (69.5), warmest Jun (73.5).
- Oddities: 82 °F on Feb 21 2026 and 81.6 on Feb 13 (likely doors open / system off on warm days), and a 57.4 °F low (probably a thermostat setback or the unit being off). Nothing to fix.

## Radon & CO2
- Radon never exceeded 1.41 pCi/L; EPA action level is 4.0, Airthings' caution level 2.7. Flat across the day; slightly higher in winter. Notable: it has been trending down since July (0.48 → Aug 0.30 → Sep 0.15).
- CO2 never reached 1,000 ppm; small morning rise (587 at 8 AM) from occupancy, lowest mid-afternoon (537).

## Best and worst months, overall
- **Best: February 2026** — driest (42.7% RH), low PM (8.3 avg but a 108 spike), normal VOC. Also Aug 2026 for particulates (4.9/5.9) but it was the most humid month.
- **Worst: June 2026** — PM2.5 15.7 avg with the year's four biggest spikes, humidity 58 avg/80 peak, VOC 341, radon high of 1.41. Runner-up **October 2025** — humidity 82% peak, VOC 3,083 spike, PM2.5 279 spike.
