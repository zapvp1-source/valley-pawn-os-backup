282 Bald Rock Road · Verona, VA Analysis · 3 September 2026

Is our pricing costing us bookings?

Short answer: not for the season we just ran — we are ahead of last year on both nights and revenue. But there is a flat $975 rate wall sitting across October through March, and it is priced 40–150% above what those same months actually cleared at last year. November and December are currently at zero.

Applied 3 Sep

The rate wall is gone. Every night from 5 November through 31 March is repriced and live, confirmed on Airbnb, and June and July 2027 now carry a real peak curve including a July 4th premium. Each night was read back off the calendar to verify. Minimum stay is 2 nights everywhere, holidays included. The owner block on 1–4 Nov was left untouched, and August 2027 was held on purpose. VRBO has taken the shoulder rate but not yet the holiday windows; a re-check is scheduled for the morning of 4 Sep.

The three findings

What the data says

No

Price is not suppressing demand in-season. On the same calendar day last year we had 21 nights and $16,881 on the books for Sep–Dec. Today we have 24 nights and $24,093 — more nights, 43% more revenue, at a 25% higher average rate.

No

Guests who see the listing are not balking at the price. Airbnb booking conversion for August was 6.71% against 0.46% for comparable listings in the area — roughly 14× the market. One in five people who open the listing page books it.

Yes

Price is blocking the off-season. Every open night from October 2026 through March 2027 is priced at a flat $975 weekday / $1,435–$1,549 weekend, with no seasonal curve, no holiday premium and no last-minute discount. Those months cleared at $400–$799 a night last year.

Those four numbers together rule out a price objection and point at something else. A listing with no open inventory stops appearing in search, which is why page views collapsed to 2–4 in the weeks where the calendar was full. We are getting half the market's traffic and converting fourteen times better on it. That is a visibility constraint, not a price ceiling.

One number does cut the other way: August 2026 sold the same 22 nights as August 2025, but at $691 a night versus $746 — a 7% lower rate at identical occupancy. Peak August was left slightly cheap.

The sharpest example is Christmas. The week of 21–28 December 2025 sold as a seven-night stay at $520 a night. The same week in 2026 is on the calendar at $975 weekday and $1,435 on Christmas Day — and has no bookings. Both numbers are probably wrong: last year's was a giveaway for a ten-person mountain house with a pool and hot tub over the holidays; this year's is a flat rate that happens to land there by accident rather than by decision.

One correction worth recording: summer 2027 was not underpriced in the way the August-2026 number suggested. Three 2027 stays had already booked at $976–$1,435 a night, eleven months out, at the flat asking rate — so the market clearly pays it. What summer lacked was structure: no July 4th premium, and a soft August priced identically to a peak July Saturday. That is what these four windows fix.

Do this

Six changes, in order

  1. Break the flat rate for Nov 2026 – Mar 2027Apply the season curve above in Guesty so it pushes to both Airbnb and VRBO. This is the single change that matters; everything below is refinement.
  2. Price the three holidays as holidaysThanksgiving, Christmas week and New Year each get their own rate and minimum stay. Right now all three carry the same price as a random weekday in February.
  3. Turn on a last-minute ladderOff-season only: 15% off inside 21 days, 25% off inside 7 days. Off-season stays here book on a 32-day median lead time, so the last three weeks are where those nights are won or lost.
  4. Add an orphan-gap ruleDrop the minimum to 1 night and discount 20% on any gap of two nights or fewer between bookings. Last year's two lowest-rate stays — $353 and $320 a night — were exactly these gaps, filled by hand at a loss.
  5. Raise the summer floorAugust sold 100% of available nights at 7% below last year. Peak weeks are underpriced, not overpriced — the flat rate cuts both ways.
  6. Evaluate Guesty PriceOptimizerIt is not currently enabled, which is why none of the above is happening automatically. A manual curve solves this winter; automation stops it recurring.

Last November through March delivered 30 nights and $19,620, excluding the 30-night corporate stay. A curve that recovers last year's occupancy and lifts the rate modestly puts that window near $30,000 — roughly $11,000 of upside against a calendar that today has zero nights booked in two of those five months.
