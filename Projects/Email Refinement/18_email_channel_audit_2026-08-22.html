Email Channel Audit

Where the email program actually stands after the 12-week engine, and the ten things holding it back.

August 22, 2026 · Verified against live Brevo API, DNS, and per-link click data

The verdict. The plumbing is genuinely good — better than most businesses this size will ever build. Call and text clicks are tracked through real redirects, every send carries UTMs, a preflight watchdog blocks uninstrumented campaigns, seed addresses ride every send, and the Deal of the Week has a full prompt → chase → pick → send chain that managers actually feed. None of that is the problem.

The problem is that all of it is pointed at the wrong audience, on a cadence that broke three weeks ago, over a sending domain that is quietly failing authentication. Since June 1 the channel has delivered 110,983 emails and produced 70 measurable calls or texts. That is 0.63 per 1,000 against a target of 8, and against a 12-week goal of 10.

Root cause 1 — The audience split

Finding 01 · highest impact

The best content we make reaches 1.4% of the list

Deal of the Week is the strongest asset in the whole marketing stack. Managers submit 10 of 10 weeks. It has a Monday prompt, an 11 AM chase, a 12:30 picker, and a Thursday self-heal watchdog. It is the one piece of content with real merchandise, real prices, and a real reason to walk in.

It sends to list 7 — 194 people. The other 13,032 contacts receive exactly one email a month: the Gold & Silver blast, which is close to the same email every month.

This happened for a defensible reason. In June, segment-targeted sends returned all-zero stats from the Brevo API, so the fix was to send to a maintained "Engaged" list instead. That fixed measurement. It also shrank the weekly audience by 98%. We traded reach for a readable number, and then optimized the number.

Finding 02

The "engaged" list is partly machines, and one headline number is an artifact

W8 Harrisonburg (July 23) reported 250 calls + texts per 1,000 — a figure quoted this morning in the social audit as the reason to resume weekly sends on Store Spotlight. It does not survive inspection:

  • Brevo reports 193 clickers on 142 delivered. More clickers than recipients is not possible for humans.
  • In a Harrisonburg spotlight, the Harrisonburg call button got 2 clicks. Roanoke's got 6. Waynesboro's got 5. Lexington's text got 5. That is a scanner walking the footer directory, not a Harrisonburg customer picking up the phone.
  • The other three store spotlights — Culpeper, Waynesboro, Roanoke — produced 0 calls and 0 texts each. Store Spotlight is 1-for-4, and the 1 is noise.

Worse, the automation that maintains the engaged list is "any link click → add to list 7." That rule recruits security scanners into the core audience. It is why list 7 grew from 128 to 194 while real engagement stayed flat, and why list-7 sends report click rates of 58–136% of delivered.

Consequence: resuming weekly on Store Spotlight is still a reasonable call, but not because of that number. Decide it on merit, and rebuild the engaged list on a definition machines can't satisfy.

Root cause 2 — The deliverability floor

Finding 03 · fixable this week

The sending domain has two SPF records, which means it has none

fcfpawn.com publishes two separate v=spf1 TXT records:

  • v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all
  • v=spf1 include:dc-aa8e722993._spfm.fcfpawn.com ~all

Per RFC 7208 §4.5, a domain publishing more than one SPF record produces a PermError — receiving servers must treat SPF as unevaluable rather than picking one. Every message we send is being judged with one of its three authentication legs broken. DKIM is correctly configured (brevo1/brevo2 selectors resolve), so mail still authenticates on DKIM alignment — but we are shipping a permanent red flag to Gmail and Yahoo on every send, and Gmail is 64% of the file.

Fix: merge into one record — v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:dc-aa8e722993._spfm.fcfpawn.com ~all — and delete the other. Ten minutes in GoDaddy DNS.

Finding 05

We send from a domain customers have never seen

Every campaign goes out from jdavis@fcfpawn.com. Customers know thevalleypawn.com. They have never heard of Full Circle Finance. A personal-looking address at an unrecognized domain is one of the strongest "this is spam" signals a human can act on, and it is showing up in the sender line of every one of the 110,983 emails we sent this summer.

thevalleypawn.com is registered in Brevo but shows authenticated: false, verified: false — the DKIM records were never added. DMARC on both domains is p=none with reports going only to Brevo's aggregator, so nobody here has ever seen a DMARC report.

Fix: authenticate thevalleypawn.com in Brevo, move the sender to something like hello@thevalleypawn.com with the friendly name Valley Pawn, and set reply-to per-store so a reply reaches the store instead of Joshua's inbox.

Root cause 4 — The channel does one thing, and it's the least valuable thing

Finding 07 · biggest untapped revenue

There is no triggered email at all — including the one email a pawn shop should never be without

Every send in the account's history is a broadcast. Nothing fires off a customer's actual behavior. Missing entirely:

  • Loan due in 5 days → pay online. This is the highest-ROI email in the pawn industry. It directly lifts interest collection, cuts forfeitures, and it is welcome mail, not marketing — people want the reminder. Bravo holds every due date.
  • Grace period ending / last chance before forfeiture. Same mechanic, higher urgency.
  • Layaway payment due — and layaway season starts in three weeks.
  • Forfeited-loan win-back. Brevo list 11 exists, the attributes exist (FORFEITED_LOAN, LAST_FORFEIT_DATE, FORFEIT_STORE) — and the list holds zero contacts. Built, never populated. Someone who just lost an item to forfeiture is a proven, high-intent customer.
  • Welcome series. No welcome campaign and no welcome template exist in the account. New contacts wait up to 30 days for their first email — the monthly gold blast — by which point they've forgotten us.
  • Birthday. Documented in valley-pawn-context as a live template with a 20% offer. It does not exist, and no birthday field exists on any contact.
  • Giveaway entrants — 82 people who raised their hand in July and got no follow-up.

Broadcast email at 1% open rate is a hard way to make money. Triggered email routinely runs 40–60% open and 10× the revenue per send, because it arrives when the customer already has the subject on their mind.

Finding 09

Store segments were built a month ago and have never been used

Segments 7–11 (Culpeper, Harrisonburg, Lexington, Roanoke, Waynesboro) were created July 22. Every send since has gone to list 3 or list 7. A Culpeper customer receives a Harrisonburg spotlight with a Harrisonburg address and five stores' worth of phone buttons. The relevance cost is obvious and the segments to fix it already exist.

Finding 10

Nothing has ever been tested, and the creative asks for nothing

  • Zero A/B tests. abTesting: false on every campaign in the account's history. sendAtBestTime: false. Every send is Thursday 10 AM. Not one hypothesis has ever been tested.
  • The subject lines don't say anything. "What's new at Valley Pawn Roanoke." "Heading back to school? Start here." No number, no price, no deadline, no reason to open now. The two most recent gold subject lines are near-duplicates of each other.
  • The logo is the most-clicked element in almost every send — 15 clicks on the August blast, 8 on W8, more than any offer or CTA. When the header beats the offer, the offer isn't an offer.
  • Twenty CTA buttons per email. Five stores × call/text × two placements. The duplicate _top_call and _top_text variants have received 3 clicks total across every campaign ever sent — they cannibalize the footer set and add choice overload. Twenty equally weighted options reliably produce fewer actions than one.
  • No urgency mechanic outside the holiday 15%. Nothing expires. Nothing is scarce. Nothing is priced. "Gold is at $X/gram this week — bring it in before Saturday" is a different email than "we buy gold."

Finding 11 · housekeeping

Bad addresses are entering continuously

121 hard bounces in July, 64 in August. Hard bounces are auto-suppressed after the first occurrence, so these are new invalid addresses each month — almost certainly typed at the counter and imported through the Chekkit/Bravo path with no validation. 15.9% of the file is already suppressed. A syntax check plus an MX check at import would stop most of it, and a tablet-facing "type your email, we'll text you the receipt" flow would stop the rest.

Three decisions that are genuinely yours

  1. Sender identity. Moving from jdavis@fcfpawn.com to hello@thevalleypawn.com is right for the brand but resets some sender reputation. Recommendation: do it, warm over two weeks, starting with the engaged tier only.
  2. Loan reminders. These are transactional messages about someone's active loan. They need a short legal read on notice language and opt-out handling before the first send. Recommendation: build it, hold the launch until that read is done.
  3. How wide to go on the dormant 11,000. Options are (a) keep mailing everyone monthly and accept a 1% open rate, (b) rotate a fifth of them into each weekly send to re-warm gradually, or (c) sunset properly. Recommendation: (b) — it re-warms without the optics of a smaller list, and it is reversible.

What's working — don't touch it

  • Call/text redirect instrumentation on thevalleypawn.com. Genuinely clever, and the reason this audit could measure anything.
  • The brevo-preflight-watchdog and the internal-seeds standing rule. Both are doing real work.
  • The Deal of the Week submission mechanic — 10/10 manager participation is the hardest thing in this whole stack and it already works.
  • Unsubscribe and complaint rates. Well inside every threshold, with room to send more, not less.
