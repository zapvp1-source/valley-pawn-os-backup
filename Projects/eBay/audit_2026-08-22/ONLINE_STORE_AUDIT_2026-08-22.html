Online Store Audit

All 514 live eBay listings across 5 stores, 555 sales from the last 90 days, and thevalleypawn.com — pulled live from the eBay Trading API and the site itself.

The online channel is doing $82,064 every 90 days — about $328K/year. It is also badly misallocated, running with the brakes on at its best-performing store, and leaking its highest-margin path entirely.

Three things are true at once: the machine works, the inventory is in the wrong place, and the settings are costing money on every single order. Nothing here needs new software. Almost all of it is configuration and where product gets pointed.

The five findings that matter

01 Culpeper holds 60% of the inventory and produces 31% of the revenue Biggest single lever

Revenue per active listing over 90 days: Lexington $433, Waynesboro $361, Harrisonburg $359, Roanoke $173, Culpeper $82. Lexington earns 5.2× per listing what Culpeper does — and has 27 listings live against Culpeper's 307.

Waynesboro, Harrisonburg and Lexington together hold 20% of the listings and generate 47% of the revenue. Their sell-through is 64–69% per quarter; Culpeper's is 42%. These three stores are not demand-constrained, they are supply-constrained — they are selling nearly everything they put up and then running out.

FixStop treating listing volume as a Culpeper activity. Set a floor of 100 active listings per store and route aged Bravo stock from every location to the store account that actually turns it. At even half their current revenue-per-listing, filling WAY/HAR/LEX to 100 each is on the order of +$38K per quarter.

02 Lexington is still Below Standard — and eBay has paused its ads Verified live today

The Aug 20 evaluation did not clear it. Seller Hub right now reads Current seller level: Below Standard, next evaluation Sep 20. Late shipment rate 4.23% against a 3% ceiling; 2 transaction defects; 2 cases closed without resolution. Tracking upload is a clean 100% — this is purely a ship-speed problem.

The consequence is worse than the fee penalty. The Advertising dashboard carries an explicit banner: "your advertising campaigns have been paused." Promoted Listings for the last 31 days: 0 clicks, $0 sales, $0 ad spend. Listing views are down 22.1% period over period. The store with the best sell-through in the company has its visibility engine switched off.

Also sitting there: 528 unread Seller Hub messages and 26 items ended and never relisted — against 27 active. Half of Lexington's potential inventory is dead on the floor.

FixBetween now and Sep 20, Lexington ships same-day with the label bought through eBay, every time — that is the only metric standing between it and Above Standard. Then relist the 26 unsold items and turn Promoted Listings back on. This one store is the highest-ROI fix in the audit.

03 Not one of the 514 listings qualifies for Top Rated Plus ~$4K/yr in fees

Top Rated Plus requires same-day or 1-business-day handling and 30-day free returns. Live settings across the whole estate:

  • Handling time is 2 days at four stores and 3 days at Roanoke. Never 1.
  • Return shipping is buyer-paid on every single listing that accepts returns.
  • Roanoke runs 14-day returns on 103 of its 106 listings — the only store not on 30-day, and out of step with the 30-day warranty the brand promises everywhere else.
  • 45 listings accept no returns at all (40 Culpeper, 3 Roanoke, 1 each Harrisonburg/Lexington). Those are actively downranked by eBay.

The Top Rated Plus badge carries a ~10% cut on the final value fee plus a search boost. On $328K a year that discount alone is roughly $4,300 — before counting the ranking lift, which is worth more.

FixThree bulk revisions, one afternoon: handling time to 1 day everywhere, Roanoke's 14-day returns to 30-day, and free 30-day returns on everything under ~3 lbs where the return-shipping risk is small. Fix the 45 no-return listings in the same pass.

05 The highest-margin sales channel has one item in it Structural

thevalleypawn.com pulled 10,051 views from 4,759 visitors in the last 90 days. The site has WooCommerce and WooPayments live, a working cart and checkout, and a Shop-in-Store page for buy-online/pickup-in-store. That page currently holds one product — a $2,099 Warwick bass at Harrisonburg, posted 23 July. It is the only product on the site. There have been zero website orders, ever.

The sync automation is not broken — I checked its logs, it ran cleanly at 10:11 and 16:44 today. It has nothing to sync because the stores aren't posting items to #in-store-inventory. One item in a month.

Meanwhile the main Shop page routes all 504 items straight to eBay, where roughly 13% comes off the top in fees. Every sale we could have taken on our own checkout at full margin, we hand to eBay instead.

FixThis is a people problem with a working pipeline behind it. Give each store a weekly quota — 5 items into #in-store-inventory, highest-ticket first — and the site becomes a real 25-item/week store with no fee drag. The plumbing is already paid for and running.

Aged inventory

207 listings (40%) have been live more than 90 days. 99 (19%) more than 180. 27 more than a year — carrying $10,959 of listed value that isn't moving. Culpeper owns 63 of the over-180s.

There is already an eBay Listing-Age Standard on the books as a signed company policy. It is not being enforced against the live estate — an item sitting 400 days at an unchanged price is not a pricing decision, it's an absence of one.

Website: what's actually wrong

  • The /shop/ page has no H1 and no meta description. 504 products, one URL, no Product or ItemList structured data — so it is ineligible for Google product rich results and free Shopping listings. The Organization, PawnShop and FAQ schema are all in place; product schema is the one that's missing.
  • Page weight and speed. 504 cards and 511 images on a single page; full load measured at 5.6 seconds. Pagination or lazy-rendering below the first 60 cards would cut that hard.
  • Blog cannibalisation. 46 posts, and at least a dozen are near-duplicate pairs competing for the same query — "build an emergency fund" three times, "pawn loans vs payday loans" twice, "pawn shop myths" twice, "buying pre-owned power tools" twice, "selling silver" twice. The default hello-world post is still published.
  • Two competing sets of location pages. /locations/culpeper/ (131 words, hero layout) and /culpeper/ (320 words, narrow ad-landing layout) both exist and are both indexable, for all five towns. On inspection the top-level set looks like deliberate paid-ad landing pages rather than an accident — but they're competing with the real location pages for the same local queries. They should be noindex, not deleted. Neither set is linked from the homepage navigation.
  • Good news: the nightly eBay feed is healthy (page refreshed today), and every outbound eBay link now carries campaign parameters, so click attribution is wired — that gap from the July review is closed.
