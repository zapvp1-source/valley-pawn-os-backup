# Voice clone — Take 1 (read about 3 minutes, or all of it)

Voice Memos set to Lossless. Phone 6–8 inches away, quiet soft room. Read like you're talking to a new hire at the counter. Stumble? Pause two seconds and repeat the sentence; don't stop the recording. Name the memo "Take 1".

---

Welcome to Valley Pawn. We started in 2000, and in 2015 the current owners bought the Staunton store with a plan to grow across the Valley. Today there are five stores: Culpeper, Waynesboro, Harrisonburg, Lexington and Roanoke. Every one of them is a licensed pawnbroker and a federal firearms licensee, and every one of them is Valley Pawn. One name, no matter which store you're in.

Here's the standard. Nobody gets six feet in the door without being greeted. You stand up, you say welcome to Valley Pawn, and you mean it. If you're already helping someone, you still look up and let the new person know you'll be right with them. Every customer, every visit, hears the three things that set us apart: a thirty day warranty on everything we sell, the mobile app to shop and pay, and free layaway. And remember, the thing that loses customers isn't rudeness. It's indifference. Your paycheck and mine come from the people walking through that door.

The chain of command is short. You report to your Store Manager. Store Managers report to Preston Peters, the Market Manager. Preston reports to Joshua Davis, the owner and CEO. Take questions and problems to your Store Manager first. If it isn't resolved in a reasonable time, go up one level. If the problem is about your Store Manager, it goes to Preston. And if something is very serious or private, you can go straight to Joshua.

Store hours are ten to six, and you're in the building by nine thirty to get ready. Culpeper is open Monday through Saturday. The other four stores are closed on Wednesday. Everybody's closed Sunday. And on the first and third Monday of the month there's a nine A M team meeting by Zoom from each store. That's paid time, and if you're scheduled that morning, you're there.

This is how the Academy works. Levels unlock one at a time, in order, and Level one has to be done by the end of your third day. Every lesson ends in a short test. Eighty percent or better unlocks the next one. You get two tries. If you miss twice, your manager gets a nudge to sit with you before you go again, and that's a good thing, not a punishment. Some skills can't be tested on a screen, like counting a drawer. Those lessons end with a Floor Check: your manager watches you do it for real and marks it off. Screen test plus Floor Check means you've mastered it.

You've got two documents. The Employee Handbook is the employment side: pay, time off, conduct, benefits. The Policies and Procedures Manual, which everybody calls the P and P, is how the stores actually run: cash, loans, firearms, the phone, the customer. If it's an employment question, the Handbook controls. If it's a store procedure, the P and P controls. Both are in Gusto under Documents, and you can open them any time.

When you're not sure what the rule is, your first stop is the ask handbook channel in Slack. Type the question the way you'd ask a coworker. It answers only from the Handbook and the P and P, and it tells you which section the answer came from, so you can go read it. If the rule isn't written down, it says not covered, ask your Store Manager, and that's the honest answer. It doesn't guess. It's not for things like the Wi Fi password or your schedule. And if your pay is wrong, that goes straight to Preston or Joshua, not the bot.

In Gusto you'll sign one acknowledgment that covers both documents. It says you received them, you read them, you understand them, and you agree to follow them. If anything is unclear, ask before you sign. It also says employment is at will, for you and for the company. It says the company can change its policies, and when a new version comes out you'll sign again. And it includes your duty to speak up: if you believe something breaks the law or a policy, especially around firearms, cash, or customer records, you report it, and nobody can retaliate against you for doing that.
