## ⚠️ The two things that will surprise you

1. **Permits are PER PERSON, not per store.** Va. Code §54.1-4100 defines "dealer" to include
   *"all employers and principals on whose behalf a purchase is made, and any employee or agent
   who makes any purchase for or on behalf of his employer or principal."* The letter states
   plainly: *"each person who engages in any of the acts defined... must have his/her own
   permit."* **$200 each, fingerprints each, background check each, annually.** So the real cost
   scales with how many Roanoke employees touch precious-metals buys. Worth deciding deliberately
   whether to limit PM buying authority to 1–2 trained people per shift rather than permitting the
   whole staff.
2. **The BOND is the exception — one covers everyone.** *"A single bond may be secured upon an
   employer or principal... to cover all employees and all transactions occurring at a single
   location."* So it's **one $10,000 bond for the location**, not one per person.

**Everything on this list renews ANNUALLY.** Fingerprints, background checks, scale certification,
bond, lease proof — all of it, every year.

## Other obligations once permitted

- **Business must operate from the fixed location on the application only** (Va. Code §54.1-4108).
- **Notify the chief law-enforcement officer of ALL closings and re-openings** if the business is
  not operated without interruption (Saturdays, Sundays, and recognized holidays excepted). Note
  Valley Pawn's non-Culpeper stores are **closed Wednesday & Sunday** — worth asking Teresa
  directly whether a standing weekly Wednesday closure needs to be reported, since the code's
  carve-out names only Sat/Sun/holidays.

## Penalty for operating without

Class 2 misdemeanor first offense, Class 1 misdemeanor for subsequent offenses.
