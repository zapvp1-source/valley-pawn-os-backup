There’s something about May in the Shenandoah Valley. The dogwoods and redbuds are still showing off, the mountains are green again, and every small town from Culpeper down to Roanoke seems to remember how to host an outdoor event. After a long winter, May is when the Valley comes back to life — and for our five Valley Pawn communities, the month packs more community, tradition, and small-town joy than just about any other time of year.

Farmers Markets Are Open for Business

Every one of our Valley Pawn towns has a local farmers market that either opens or hits full swing in May — Culpeper’s downtown market, Waynesboro’s Saturday gathering on Main Street, Harrisonburg’s long-running market near Turner Pavilion, Lexington’s bustling Saturday setup, and Roanoke’s flagship market downtown. Early spring means strawberries, spring greens, radishes, cut flowers, fresh bread, and the first honey of the year. If you haven’t been to your local market yet this season, pick a Saturday morning — bring cash in small bills and go early for the best picks.

Memorial Day Weekend: Remember, Then Gather

Memorial Day weekend is the unofficial kickoff to summer, but it starts with remembrance. Several Valley towns hold ceremonies at local veterans memorials and cemeteries — always worth attending if you can, even briefly. After that, it’s the first real cookout weekend of the year. If you’re short a grill, a cooler, a smoker, or outdoor tools for the yard project you’ve been putting off, the retail floor at any Valley Pawn store is worth a stop before the big weekend. In most of the country, May is just another month. In the Shenandoah Valley, it’s the month everything opens up — the markets, the trails, the festival calendars, the front porches.
