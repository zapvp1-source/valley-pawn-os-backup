Good tools are built to last. A quality drill, circular saw, or impact driver can run for years — often decades — with basic care. What to Check Before You Buy

A little inspection goes a long way. Whether you’re shopping our shelves or anywhere else, run through this quick checklist before you commit:

  • Power it up. Corded or battery, the tool should start smoothly and run without grinding, sparking, or odd smells. Listen for a steady motor.
  • Check the cord and housing. Look for frayed cords, cracked casings, or missing guards. Surface scuffs are normal; structural damage is not.
  • Test the trigger and controls. Variable-speed triggers should respond evenly, and safety switches should engage properly.
  • Ask about batteries. For cordless tools, confirm the battery holds a charge — and whether a charger is included.
  • Look for wear on moving parts. Chucks, blades, and bases should be secure, not wobbly.

Match the Tool to the Job

One last tip: buy for the project in front of you, not the one you might tackle someday. A homeowner refreshing a deck needs a solid drill and a reliable circular saw far more than the biggest, most expensive kit on the shelf. Starting with the essentials — a drill/driver, a saw, and a good measuring tape — covers the vast majority of home projects.
