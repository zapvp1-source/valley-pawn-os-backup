Why Now Is a Reasonable Time to Sell

Gold prices have stayed historically strong over the past several years, which means even small or “scrap” gold items can be worth a meaningful payout. You don’t need a heirloom-grade piece to make a sale worth your time. A handful of broken chains, a single earring whose match disappeared years ago, or a worn-out wedding band can add up faster than people expect — especially when you bring everything in at once instead of one piece at a time.

The other reason to consider selling old gold jewelry now is simpler: gold you don’t wear isn’t doing anything for you. It’s not appreciating in your sock drawer the way it would if you cashed out and put the money toward something useful — paying down a credit card, covering a car repair, or just adding to your emergency fund.

What Determines What Your Gold Is Worth

Three things drive the price any honest buyer will offer you:

Karat (purity). Gold jewelry is rarely pure. 24k is 99.9% gold, while 14k is about 58% gold and the rest is alloy metals that make the piece harder and more wearable. The higher the karat, the higher the per-gram value. Look for a stamp inside a ring band or on the clasp of a chain — “14k,” “10k,” “585,” or “750” all tell you what you have.

Weight. Gold is bought and sold by weight, usually in grams or pennyweights. A reputable buyer will weigh your items in front of you on a calibrated scale. If anyone refuses to let you see the scale, that’s your cue to leave.

The current spot price of gold. Spot price moves every day on the global market. A fair buyer offers a percentage of spot based on karat and weight — not a flat made-up number. Ask what spot is when you walk in. Any buyer worth working with will tell you.

What to Bring (and What You Don’t Need)

Round up everything you’re willing to part with, even the pieces you think are worthless. Broken chains, single earrings, bent rings, and tangled clumps are all fair game. We sort, test, and weigh each piece separately — so even a tiny charm contributes to your total.

You’ll need a valid government-issued photo ID. Virginia state law requires us to record ID for every gold purchase. You do not need an appraisal, a receipt, the original box, or any documentation about how you got the jewelry. What you do not need to bring: anxiety. There’s no obligation to sell. If you don’t like the offer, you walk out with your jewelry and a quote you can compare elsewhere.

Sell vs. Pawn: Which Makes Sense?

People sometimes walk in planning to sell and end up pawning instead — or vice versa. The difference matters:

Selling means the jewelry is ours after the transaction. You get cash today and the item is gone. This is the right move for pieces you have no emotional attachment to — broken, mismatched, or outgrown items you’d never wear again.

Pawning means we hold the jewelry as collateral for a short-term loan. You get cash today, and you have a set window (typically 30 days, with an option to renew) to pay back the loan plus interest and get the piece back. This is the better option for sentimental items — wedding rings, grandma’s bracelet, anything you’d regret losing. No credit check, no impact on your credit score either way.

A Few Tips to Get the Best Offer

Bring everything at once. Most buyers, including us, offer better per-gram rates on larger lots because we process them as a single transaction.

Don’t clean it. You don’t need to polish anything. Tarnish and grime don’t affect the gold content, and aggressive cleaning can actually damage stones or solder joints — which could matter if a piece turns out to be worth more whole than melted.

Get a second opinion if you’re not sure. A trustworthy gold buyer will not pressure you to sell on the spot. If you want to shop your offer around, that’s fair — and a confident buyer expects it.

Ask questions. How is the karat tested? What’s today’s spot price? What percentage of spot are you paying? Honest answers are the easiest test of whether you’re in the right place.
