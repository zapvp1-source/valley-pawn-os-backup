Understanding Karats and Weight

Two numbers drive the value of any gold piece: how pure it is and how much it weighs. Purity is measured in karats. Pure gold is 24 karat, while most jewelry is 14K or 18K, meaning it is mixed with other metals for durability. A 14K piece is about 58 percent gold, and an 18K piece is 75 percent gold, so an 18K chain is worth noticeably more per gram than a 14K one of the same size.

Weight is measured in grams or pennyweights. Look inside a ring band or on a clasp and you will often find a tiny stamp such as 14K, 585, 750, or 925 (925 indicates sterling silver, not gold). Those little marks tell an appraiser exactly what they are working with and help us give you an honest, transparent offer.

What Affects Your Payout

The daily gold market sets the baseline. Prices move constantly, so an offer this week may differ from one a month ago. Beyond the market, three things matter most: the karat purity, the total weight of actual gold, and whether the piece has any additional value beyond its metal — for example, a designer brand, gemstones, or collector appeal. Plain scrap gold is valued purely by weight and purity, while a signed designer bracelet may be worth more intact.

Getting the Most for Your Gold

Keep any gemstones or designer pieces separate, since they may be worth more than their gold weight alone.
