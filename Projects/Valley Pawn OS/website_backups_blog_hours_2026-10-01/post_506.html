What to Look For When Shopping Used

Not every used tool is a bargain. The good news is you don’t need to be a master mechanic to tell a workhorse from a worn-out one. A few quick checks will tell you almost everything you need to know:

  • Inspect the cord or battery housing. Frayed cords are a hard pass. On battery-powered tools, look for cracks where the pack clicks in.
  • Check the chuck on drills and impact drivers. It should grip firmly with no wobble.
  • Listen to the motor under load. A healthy motor spins up smoothly — no grinding, no hesitation.
  • Ask what’s included. Batteries, chargers, and bits make a real difference in total value.
  • Look at the wear pattern. Honest, even wear is fine. Cracks, missing pieces, or evidence of a drop are red flags.

The Best Tools to Buy Pre-Owned

Some categories are absolute no-brainers in the used market. These are the tools you should always check the pawn shop for before paying full retail:

  • Power drills, impact drivers, and combo kits — usually 40–60 percent off retail, often with batteries and chargers included.
  • Circular saws, miter saws, and reciprocating saws — simple mechanically, easy to inspect, and built to outlive their owners.
  • Air compressors and pneumatic tools — perfect for framing nailers, roofing nailers, and tire inflation around the property.
  • Generators — a fantastic buy for storm season; just run it before you leave the lot.
  • Yard equipment — string trimmers, leaf blowers, hedge trimmers, and chainsaws all hold up beautifully when they’ve been cared for.
  • Welders, grinders, and shop tools — workshop staples that rarely come up for cheap brand new.
