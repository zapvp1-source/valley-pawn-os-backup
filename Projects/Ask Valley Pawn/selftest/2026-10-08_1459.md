Virginia's one-handgun-per-30-days gate comes first, and it applies to the second and third handgun:
1. Before the second handgun, verify and record an exception, or refuse the sale. The ones you'll meet at the counter: a valid Virginia concealed handgun permit (record the permit number); a Virginia State Police multiple-handgun purchase certificate (good for 7 days, surrendered to us before the sale, kept on file 2 years); a handgun trade-in in the same transaction (one such transaction per day); replacement of a handgun stolen or lost in the last 30 days (attach the police report); or a law-enforcement officer or agency. No exception means no sale, however it's papered afterward.
2. Once the gate is cleared, a 4473 for every handgun. The customer fills Section B in their own hand. You do A, C and E, on the form itself. ID must be unexpired government photo ID, and the customer must be 21 or older for handguns.
3. Two or more pistols or revolvers to the same customer within five consecutive business days means ATF Form 3310.4, filed by close of business that day.
4. Notify the Store Manager right away, and have the customer sign the Multiple Handgun Sales Acknowledgement Form. If they refuse to sign, note it and tell the Store Manager. Keep the paperwork on file for 5 years.
5. Never structure the sales to dodge the reporting. Straw purchase red flags (one person picking the guns, another doing the paperwork, cash passing between them, "it's for my buddy") mean stop and call Preston.

In Virginia the State Police run the check (VCheck), not ATF or NICS directly, and a delay is not a denial. Here's what to do:
1. The firearm stays held in the store. It does not go out the door on a delay.
2. The State Police have until the end of the store's fifth business day, counted from when you submitted. Holidays like Dec 25 don't count.
3. What happens after that fifth business day is Preston's call. Don't make up a rule.
4. If the customer asks, say: "Let me get you the right answer from my manager." Then get it from Preston.
5. On the phone, you can discuss check status only with the buyer, after you verify who they are. Never mention a gun, an approval or an account on a voicemail.
