Good question. Three handguns at once means the Virginia gate comes first, then the federal paperwork.

1. Virginia gate: a person can't buy more than one handgun in 30 days unless an exception applies. Before the second handgun goes out, verify and record one: a valid Virginia concealed handgun permit (record the permit number), a State Police multiple-handgun certificate (good for 7 days, surrender it before the sale, we keep it 2+ years), a handgun trade-in in the same transaction (one per day), a replacement for one stolen or lost in the last 30 days (attach the police report), or a law-enforcement officer or agency. No exception means no sale of the second and third, however it's papered afterward.
2. 4473 on each gun. The customer fills out Section B in their own hand. You do A, C and E. Check an unexpired government photo ID, and the buyer must be 21 or older for handguns.
3. Tell your Store Manager right away. Two or more handguns to the same customer within five consecutive business days also means the ATF multiple-sale report (Form 3310.4), filed by close of business that day.
4. Have the customer sign the Multiple Handgun Sales Acknowledgement Form. If they refuse, note it and tell the Store Manager.
5. Keep the paperwork in the compliance file for at least 5 years, and every gun goes out with a gun lock.

Don't split the sale up to dodge the reporting. That's a federal violation and a terminable offense. And if it looks like he's buying for someone else, don't coach him, stop and call Preston.
