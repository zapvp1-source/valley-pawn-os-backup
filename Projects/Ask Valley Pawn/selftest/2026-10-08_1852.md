Three handguns at once means the Virginia gate comes first, then the federal paperwork.

1. Virginia one-handgun-per-30-days: a customer can't buy more than one handgun in 30 days unless you verify and record an exception first. If you can't, you sell one gun and refuse the others. The exceptions you'll actually see: a valid Virginia concealed handgun permit (record the permit number); a Virginia State Police multiple-handgun purchase certificate (good for 7 days, surrendered to us before the sale, kept on file at least 2 years); a handgun trade-in in the same transaction (one such transaction per day); replacing a handgun stolen or lost in the last 30 days (attach the police report copy); or a law-enforcement officer or agency. A sale that fails this gate isn't made, however it's papered afterward.
2. 4473 on each gun. The customer fills Section B in their own hand. You fill A, C and E, all on the form itself. Check an unexpired government photo ID that matches, and the customer must be 21 or older for handguns.
3. ATF Form 3310.4: two or more pistols or revolvers to the same customer within five consecutive business days. File it by close of business that day.
4. Tell your Store Manager right away. The customer signs the Multiple Handgun Sales Acknowledgement Form. If they refuse, note it and tell the Store Manager.

Don't split the sale up to dodge the reporting. That's a federal violation and a terminable offense. If it looks like one person is picking the guns and someone else is doing the paperwork, or it's "for my buddy," stop and call Preston.
