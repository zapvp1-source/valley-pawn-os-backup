Good question. Three handguns at once means the Virginia gate comes first, then the federal steps.

1. Virginia gate first. A customer can't buy more than one handgun in a 30-day period unless an exception is verified and recorded before the second handgun is sold. Otherwise no sale. The exceptions you'll actually see:
- Valid Virginia concealed handgun permit: record the permit number
- Virginia State Police multiple-handgun purchase certificate: good for 7 days, surrendered to us before the sale, kept on file at least 2 years
- Trade-in of a handgun in the same transaction (one such transaction per day)
- Replacement of a handgun stolen or lost in the last 30 days, with the police report attached
- Law enforcement officer or agency

2. Tell your store manager right away once you see it's a second handgun sale to the same customer.
3. Do the 4473 the normal way: customer fills Section B themselves, staff do A, C and E, and the ID is unexpired and matches. Handguns are 21 and up, no exceptions.
4. Two or more pistols or revolvers to the same unlicensed customer within 5 consecutive business days means ATF Form 3310.4, filed by close of business that day.
5. Have the customer sign the Multiple Handgun Sales Acknowledgement Form at the second handgun purchase. If they refuse, note it and tell the Store Manager. It doesn't replace the Virginia gate or the ATF form.
6. Keep the paperwork in the compliance file for at least 5 years.

Don't split the sale up to dodge the reporting. That's a federal violation and a terminable offense. If it looks like a straw purchase (one person picks the guns, another does the paperwork, or "it's for my buddy"), stop and call Preston. Never coach an answer.
