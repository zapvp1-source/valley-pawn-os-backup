# Scrap Bucket Comparison — Culpeper vs the other four stores (2026-10-13)

Question: Culpeper's gold scrap buckets are consistently far bigger. Is it formulaic (store size,
traffic, pricing, process) or people?

## Mix
- Karat: CUL and HAR are 14K-heavy (64% / 72% of dwt); LEX/ROA/WAY are ~50/50 10K and 14K.
  CUL also has the most 18K (6%). Higher-karat intake is a market signal (nicer jewelry walking in),
  not a buying choice.
- Culpeper writes most gold buys under jewelry categories ("Lady's Stone Ring", "Gold Pendant",
  "Gold Chain") rather than "Gold-Scrap" (17% of dwt in scrap categories vs LEX 39%, WAY 32%) —
  it buys ordinary jewelry at scrap price; LEX/WAY mostly buy what customers already call scrap.
- Day of week: CUL's buys spike Mon (256 dwt) and Tue (224) — 55% of its buy weight on two days.
  Other stores peak Thu. Culpeper runs a Monday/Tuesday gold routine the others do not.

## Verdict: formulaic, with one person-shaped amplifier
1. Culpeper's advantage is almost entirely outright gold BUYS, at the same piece size, at a
   LOWER and tighter price (~39% of melt, flat across karat, no stone premium). Nothing in the
   pricing is "paying up" — the formula is cheaper than HAR/WAY.
2. The buys come in 2x the frequency. The market is part of it (more and higher-karat jewelry
   walks in; gold is 31% of everything Culpeper takes in vs 15–23% elsewhere), and the Dowden
   split shows the location itself roughly doubles a buyer's volume.
3. The rest is process: a flat scrap formula everyone at Culpeper applies the same way (lowest
   spread), jewelry bought at scrap price under jewelry categories, a Mon/Tue gold cadence, and a
   bucket that is filled every month without fail (15% swing vs 29–50%).
4. Sandi is the amplifier — the highest-volume buyer in the company by a wide margin — but she
   is executing the same formula at the same price as her co-workers, not a different one.

What is reproducible elsewhere: the flat ~39%-of-melt scrap offer with no karat/stone drift
(HAR and WAY are paying 45–52% on stone pieces and 10K and still buying less), buying ordinary
jewelry at scrap price instead of waiting for self-declared "scrap", and closing/posting the
bucket on a fixed monthly cadence.
