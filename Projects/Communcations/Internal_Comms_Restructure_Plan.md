## The verdict

No — the current volume is not wise. The channels are publishing data, not communication. Evidence from #store-performance:

- **Near-zero engagement.** Weeks of 50-number category dumps get 0–1 reactions. The only substantive staff reply in 2 months was Sandi's *"numbers appear to be off"* — a trust problem, not a readership problem.
- **Duplicate delivery.** The 8/24 rankings posted twice (9:32 AM and 10:45 AM). In July the bot AND Joshua posted the same report on back-to-back days. Staff learn to skim past it all.
- **Corrections erode credibility.** Three "CORRECTION / disregard the earlier post" cycles since June. Once a number is publicly wrong, every future wall of numbers gets ignored.
- **Wrong altitude for the audience.** A floor employee can't act on "Layaway Balance: $92,383.14 company-wide." They can act on "Lexington: your focus this week is loan writes."

The data isn't the problem — the format, frequency, and audience targeting are.

## Operating principles

1. **Push decisions, pull data.** Slack gets only what someone must *act on or celebrate*. Full tables live in linked Sheets/canvases for whoever wants them.
2. **One number, one takeaway, one ask** per team-facing post. Anything longer goes in a thread or a link.
3. **Audience tiers.** Leadership (Joshua/Preston/Lainie) keeps full detail in private channels. Store teams get scoreboard + focus items only.
4. **Never post the same report twice.** One owner per report (automation OR manual, not both). One verified number set before anything publishes.

## The new structure

**Daily → one post.** Merge clock-in, funds verification, and intake-margin into a single "Morning Ops Digest" in one ops channel: 5 lines, exceptions only ("all clear" gets one line). Anything green is silent.

**Weekly → one scoreboard.** Replace the 8-category rankings wall with a 5-line scorecard: each store's rank, its one biggest mover, and one focus item. Full category table becomes a linked Sheet or Slack canvas, updated in place — not reposted. Add one recognition callout per week (the "Gg Culpepper!" reply proves competition is the hook — feed it).

**Monthly → one narrative recap.** Three paragraphs: what happened, what it means, what we're doing. Numbers verified against Bravo once, before posting. This replaces the multi-view analytics dumps in team-facing channels; the full 3-view analysis stays in #company-performance (leadership).

**Channel consolidation.** ~15 report channels is too many surfaces. Keep: one ops digest channel, #store-performance (scoreboard only), #company-performance (leadership), #google-reviews, #policy-announcements. Action-queue channels (#items-to-price, #items-to-markdown, #supply-request) stay — those are workflows, not reports. Fold the rest (#weekly-returns-summary, #gold-trend, #first-payment-default, #ebay-performance, etc.) into the weekly scoreboard thread or leadership channels.

## Implementation sequence (all additive — Rule #4)

3. Move monthly analytics walls out of team channels; write the narrative recap format.
4. Archive the folded channels with a pinned pointer to where the info now lives.
