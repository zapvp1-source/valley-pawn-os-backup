- Fixed today: one miskeyed answer (Level 2 lunch-time drawer balance); legal corrections on texting versus calling, the ATF two-handgun report, the 21-for-handguns rule, Virginia caller-ID rules, background-check validity and eBay's late-shipment threshold; one loan timeline everywhere (day 1 is the day after the due date; grace through day 15; hold through day 30; pull from day 31, finished by day 61; past 61 needs Joshua); one discount rule everywhere (30% ceiling, 10% the normal give, nothing past 50%); retired rules, "starting in October" wording and named-person attributions removed.

## 2. What changed, by level

**Level 1 — Your first three days.** The welcome is now a trainer introduction rather than Joshua in first person, with a three-question ungraded preview. A new "what a pawn loan is" slide gives the 30-day term, service charge, and "pick it up, extend it, or we keep it and that's the end" in plain words. The texting lesson now separates calls (allowed 18 months after the last transaction) from marketing texts (always need recorded opt-in). The phone lesson lost its dated call-review stories, gained the police-caller callback rule, and no longer commits to a loan sight unseen. The cash lesson explains how excess goes to the safe, how cash moves between drawers (Transfer Tender), and the counterfeit pen check. Goldilocks was trimmed for a day-3 hire: one discount rule, no clock times, no system history, and a cumulative scenario question.

**Level 2 — Opening, closing, cash and counts.** The miskeyed lunch-balance question was fixed. The two closing sequences were reduced to one (lock the door, Close Till, count total funds, Close Store), stated once in the closing lesson and referenced from drawer balancing. The bank run moved to the safe-and-funds lesson; the two Goldilocks slides were merged into one that leads with the rule ("every dollar that arrives is entered in Bravo the same day, before close"). "Take it on a ticket" is now explained. Jargon (NCO-ready, card reconciliation, designee) was defined or cut, and the jewelry count uses "the standard count sheet" rather than a named template.

**Level 3 — Writing and servicing a pawn loan.** Day 61 was restored to the pull timeline, first-payment defaults are taught as "no 30-day courtesy hold, but not pulled before the 15-day grace", and the rate rule is now one sentence everywhere ("you never change a rate or a fee; exceptions only through the Market Manager, entered in Bravo"). The four Virginia charges are narrated in one breath with the tier table on screen; the police report got its own slide; the intro now defines the escalation ladder and says the loan-amount decision is Level 4's job. Retired figures (flat 12.5%, the $2 lost-ticket fee, the 75-day cushion) and the "four versions in one week" call recap are gone. The send-off is four things: warranty, clearance, free layaway, app.

**Level 4 — Buy or loan, pricing and ownership.** A new "math behind every offer" slide and a worked pressure-washer example teach the 50% target and the 30% flag, with a scenario question to match. "What we pass on" was split into "never, by rule" and "passes we've learned". The pull-list lesson counts from the day after the due date, drops the 75-day cushion, and says a buy only shows on the pricing list after its hold is up. The counter-discount rule matches Level 1 (30% ceiling; fresh stock stays near the 10% the tag was built with). The military-ID exception, the statement-of-ownership gloss and the "60 days, not ever" line were added; three lessons gained a two-sentence close.

**Level 5 — Gold, silver and diamonds.** The acid method is now a procedure (scratch under plating or notch the shank, match the acid to the stamp, holds / fades-and-browns / green), with a new stamp-literacy slide (417/585/750/916/999 and the plated marks). Coins and bars are weighed and measured against published specs. The purity table (10K 0.4167 through 24K) is on screen, the melt formula is narrated in one line, and the "50% for all bullion" sentence was replaced with "the percentage comes from the spot sheet". Diamonds gained "what the tester can't tell you" (lab-grown and moissanite, loupe the girdle for an LG inscription) and a millimeter-to-carat sizing slide. The counterfeit lesson was rebuilt around new tells (tungsten bars, fake slabs, plated-over-base, hollow chains, 925 on plated) instead of repeating lesson 1.

**Level 6 — Firearms.** New slide on the federal two-handguns-in-five-business-days report (Form 3310.4, redemptions count, by close of business), plus the 30-day validity of a background check and Section D recertification, the 24-hour ATF trace-request rule, and police seizures recorded as a disposition within seven days. The handgun age slide now rests on federal law with no dated news references. Third-party redemption and the phone rule now read the same in every lesson ("your check is finished, you can come in"; never approved, denied, the gun, or on the premises). The 4473 checks were split into ID-and-form, age, and one-handgun-a-month; the VSP consent form is named once (SP-65); "pledgor" is defined on first use; all "real call last month" stories are now timeless examples.

**Level 7 — Selling (lessons 0–15).** The "four things" list (30-day warranty, clearance, free layaway, mobile app) is identical in every lesson that mentions it. Discount authority has its own slide (30% limit, 10% normal give, counter inside your limit before involving a manager, nothing past 50%). The texting slide now states the two legal rules plus the company 18-month rule, and texts go through Chekkit only. Layaway tiers and the term ceiling are separate slides, as are returns (7 days cash, 8–30 credit) and warranty (it stopped working: exchange or credit). eBay pricing uses sold used comps; the late-shipment line is "more than 3% late loses Top Rated". In lessons 8–15, every named employee, store, dollar stat and "Preston said" was replaced by the rule in the company voice, the confirm-back step and the jewelry budget question were added, the firearm-sale lesson now hands anything that feels off to Level 6, and the role-play slide is ten marks with three starred as required. Every lesson ends with a "try it on your next shift" line.

**Level 8 — Advanced and annual lessons.** Title pawns are neutral on who keeps the vehicle and point to "the valuation source posted at the counter", with Market Manager sign-off before paperwork and Joshua's approval at the end. Watches gained two real content slides (reference and serial checks, a start-to-finish intake walk) and an Apple Watch no-take list that lives at the counter. Harassment now covers customers and vendors. Customer information attributes the statutory breach clocks correctly and adds the FTC 30-day rule. Calling and texting states the Virginia law without a wrong amendment year, requires first and last name plus "Valley Pawn" on every call, and explains that the 18-month window covers calls, never texts. Handbags say "photograph and send it up before you quote" and that selling a fake is a crime. The three annual lessons open with a retrieval question from last year's rule.

**Manager Track.** Roles are defined once (Preston, Market Manager; Joshua, CEO). The weekly loan review is gone: layaways and first-payment defaults are published weekly, loans are worked in Bravo daily on the one timeline (grace to 15, hold to 30, pull 31–61, past 61 Joshua's written approval, renewal resets the due date). Aged general merchandise is "25% off and into clearance; deeper is a manager's call and stops at 50%". The discount lesson gained "your five minutes a day" and a 31–50% needs-a-manager line. The huddle lesson defines quote, capture and verification rate, adds the one-on-one coaching model and a 15-minute length, and routes conduct items to Preston the same morning. Onboarding gained a "first-week rhythm" slide; the Floor Check lesson gained a redo script and a rule for steps the shift never presents. Each lesson closes with one task for this week.
