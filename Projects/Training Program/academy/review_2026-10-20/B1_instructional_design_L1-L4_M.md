## Verdict

**Launch after fixes.** The instructional design is fundamentally sound: one objective per lesson, scenario-dominant assessment (222 of 249 items), worked-example-then-faded-practice where it matters (L2-05 reconciliation, L4-03 offer math), useful spaced repetition of the money rules across levels, and manager lessons that give real decision practice. Nothing in scope teaches a wrong law.

It cannot go to staff as it stands because four things a learner will hit in the first three days contradict each other or reality: the #ask-goldilocks rename versus L1-07's "Goldilocks doesn't answer questions" (X1), Saturday hours (X3), three-versus-four talking points (X4), and the police-report routing and Close-Till naming that differ between Level 1/2 and Level 3 (X5, X6). Two latent pipeline defects would break the course silently (X11 video-replaces-slides; X12 mislabeled clip), and the Manager Track teaches a first-payment-default rule without the statutory floor (X10) and a phone gate the associate lessons already violate (X9). All of these are cheap: roughly 1,200 characters of re-recorded narration in total, the rest is bullet/why/JSON edits.

The one genuine blocker is not in the lessons: the course narrates an Academy (locked levels, manager taps, nudges, scorecard-by-name) that the Free-plan portal cannot run (P1). Either the Grow plan and the two core agents are live before Joshua's walkthrough, or six narration sentences are re-recorded to match a manual process. Decide that first; everything else in this file is a half-day of edits plus one short re-record session.
